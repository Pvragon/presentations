Dimir Zombie Packages

The best cards for each job in three layers: universal cards any deck can run, blue-black cards, and cards that are best in Dimir Zombie tokens. Each card lists the conditions a deck must meet for it to be worth a slot. Cards are judged on merit, whenever they were printed.

How these were built

From every card, in layers

  • Universal means a colourless colour identity, so any deck can run it. Blue-black means the card needs blue or black. Dimir Zombie tokens means the card is best because the deck is wide Zombies.
  • Each package's pool is every Commander-legal blue-black card that Deckwright's role store (its card-text parser) tags for that job, or that matches the package's card-text query for the Zombie-plan packages. House rules apply: every creature is a Zombie (Gisa and Geralf excepted), and no tutors for combo pieces.
  • Every pool was split into subcategories by card type, cost and effect, and each subcategory's candidates were read, so no card falls between buckets. The strength model ranks the pools but is not yet context-aware (C22 in the strength run), so the final picks are mine, made with the card text read.
  • Prices are the cheapest playable paper printing; memorabilia, digital and non-English printings are excluded (reported to Deckwright as C32). Card images are playable printings too.
  • The deck tags show which of the six Zombie decks holds the card: C current (the old Gisa and Geralf list), H Horde, Hb Horde budget, O Onslaught, Ob Onslaught budget, M Monday.
Final review, 2026-11-01

Packages against the decks

Every pool was swept again against every legal card. Zombie-text and drain sweeps found cards the earlier card-text queries missed, the mana base became its own package from Deckwright's land fill order, and two colourless cards moved to the universal layer. Then each package was compared with the five decks.

  • 130 of 299 package cards are in none of the six decks, 45 of them owned. Being in a package meant a card was found, not that a deck weighed it. Urza's Incubator and Semblance Anvil are examples: listed, never compared with what the decks run.
  • The decks fill jobs unevenly. Only the current deck and Monday run a one-shot draw spell, one each, and the same two run one big rock each. The current deck runs three cost reducers; the new builds run one or none. Board wipes run two or three per deck. The new decks were built plan-first and the generic jobs got what was left.
  • The current deck has 32 nonland cards in no package. Many are the self-mill and graveyard-casting cards Gisa and Geralf feed on (Buried Alive, Forbidden Alchemy, Mire Triton, Laboratory Drudge, Secrets of the Dead). No package covers that job yet.
  • Horde and Onslaught run seven fetch lands each in a two-colour deck, where the mana-base rule says fetches only earn their price in three or more colours.
  • Josu Vess, Lich Knight left the token package in this review (10 mana for the tokens) and is still in four decks: current, Onslaught, Onslaught budget and Monday.
  • Every universal and Dimir Zombie card one of the five new builds skips now has a deck verdict on its row: Add (a candidate the deck update weighs against its weakest card in that package), Bench (a fine card that loses to what the decks run) or Out (wrong for these decks). The blue-black layer gets the same pass next.
  • Next, each deck gets a slot target per package and fills it from the package in rank order, recording why each package card it skips lost its slot.

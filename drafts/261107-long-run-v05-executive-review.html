v0.5 rebuilds the harness around one small loop that repeats at every size, and takes the question “are we getting anywhere?” away from the session doing the work. Nothing is built yet. Recommended: build the first proving step (three bounded runs), then judge again.

Long-run runs AI sessions for hours or days to plan, build, check and release work. The owner is the human who sets goals and makes the calls; the operator is the AI assistant that runs the harness for the owner. v0.4 reworked far longer than it built: Deckwright took 5.3 hours to build and 34 to rework, and waystar spent 62 hours checking billing history without producing the workbook the owner wanted.

Nine principles shape the design

  • One loop at every size: test, build or split, test again.
  • Done is a checklist frozen before building, written by non-builders from outside sources.
  • The deliverable never waits on a check of history.
  • Start flat; split only when outgrown.
  • Plan strategy up front, tasks just in time.
  • The CEO pane proposes, the supervisor rules, code enforces; the owner signs the goal and their typed word binds at once.
  • Code does everything mechanical, spending no tokens.
  • Speed is a core metric.
  • The harness improves only between runs.

Each version runs one loop, and only the build step builds

The owner signs the goal and capabilities; the supervisor signs the rest of the frame and samples the checklist before it freezes. Version 1 holds at most 300 cases. Passed work is never re-checked. Only the supervisor’s approval releases a version.

The same loop nests from a task to a business

A piece’s goal, checks and budget freeze at its start. An oversized piece splits into about five children, which start once their dependencies pass. Finished pieces’ checks guard later work.

Claude judges, code runs the mechanics, the owner decides

At level 1, code rules stand in for Jev until it agrees with them past a level the owner sets. Only code records a ruling, and an owner’s ruling must quote the owner’s own typed message.

Eight prompts, each a fresh session launched by code

The larger Claude model, Opus, runs the planners, the supervisor and level-1 builders; the smaller, Sonnet, runs the CEO pane, customer tester and reviewer. The level-2 improver has no model yet. Settings change only between runs, one at a time, except a few the supervisor may move within set ranges.

Plans and briefs stay small

  • Frame: about 300 lines and 12 capabilities.
  • Re-plan: twice the estimate, or two rounds without progress.
  • Supervisor plan review: every 8 active hours and before release.
  • Starting brief: builder 29k tokens, supervisor 33k, ceiling 63k.
  • Context: notice at 150k, replaced after 250k; flat builder 500k.
  • Spend without landing: stop at 3M weighted tokens, hard stop at 6M.

Problems climb a five-step ladder

The supervisor may defer up to a tenth of a version’s cases (CEO-proposed cuts count) or ask the owner for one working session. Only the owner cuts freely or lifts a stop. A piece failing after two redraws is paused and the owner told. A builder may call a test wrong once per test per version.

Only time and money without progress wake the supervisor

On last week’s logs, failure-count alarms fired on healthy runs; time and cost alarms never did. The watchdog checks every 30 minutes.

  • Spend: twice the budget.
  • Tokens per finished piece: 2.25 times the run average.
  • Blocked on another piece: over 2 hours.
  • Cost per passing case: 3.1 times the median.
  • Pass-fail flips: over 6 per version.

After a carry-on ruling, alarms restart from it; a third on one piece goes to the owner. Coordination over 30% of usage is a notice; planning targets 15%. One Deckwright session wasted an estimated 31% to 40% of 188.6M weighted tokens.

The harness changes only between runs

Each confirmed fault becomes a regression test, and one change ships per version. The operator merges defect fixes; changes to done, thresholds or the judge need the owner. The improver stays in shadow until about 8 failing and 14 healthy runs exist, 5 to 10 weeks away.

Four mechanisms carry the design

  • Frozen checklist: a case closes only by failing, then passing.
  • Supervisor: rules from the frame and the owner’s past rulings.
  • Merge train: about 4 minutes per landing; session limits set speed.
  • Flat default: rebuilt a v0.4 goal in 13 minutes versus 2.2 hours, but self-graded, so level 1 tests both ways.

Sixteen calls are pending with the owner

Already settled: two layers; no branch overlap; Opus builders; 63k-token pack budgets; usage-weighted coordination; the operator redraws endless goals under the owner’s daily review; speed as a core metric.

Testing held the core, but alarms miss the 3-hour target

  • Seven attempts to break the design failed.
  • Time and cost alarms caught 4 of 5 failing split runs at 3.0 to 6.3 hours, with no false alarms.
  • On waystar the supervisor would have ruled at hour 5 or 6, not 63.
  • Alarm settings rest on one week of runs, and flat results on one sample.

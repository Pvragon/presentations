01 Overview

Riffle Room brand book

Proposed, not yet approved · Pvragon · 10 November 2026

From the decklist to the deck box.

Riffle Room is a proposed umbrella brand from Pvragon for card players who build their own decks. It covers the hour between the decklist and the deck box. The tools are free, need no account and explain what they recommend. The shop sells printables, supplies and sealed product, shows the price per card or per sheet on paper goods, and never lists sealed product above MSRP. Magic is the first room; Lorcana and Pokémon are reserved for later rooms in the same house. Every name, colour and rule is a proposal the owner has not yet approved.

An alternate swaps in by redrawing the wordmark

Brand idea, colour, type, mark, grid, voice and architecture are identical across all five names. The table records what each wordmark has to do and the few places where a name pushes on something else.

NameWordmark moveWhat else shifts
Paper & PodAmpersand drawn with tab chamfersNothing; the Supplies line keeps its name
TucksteadShared ck join; tab tittle unusedTab mark reads as a tuck flap
Bring a DeckSmall “a”, stacked defaultLines stand alone in navigation
Seventh CardTab mark over the “v” as its tittleNothing; no imagery or line rule needed
  • Paper & Pod. The ampersand becomes the signature glyph, the way the double f is for Riffle Room, with its terminals cut at the tab’s 45-degree shoulder. The Supplies line keeps its name (D-20), so no line is renamed. “Pod” is Commander-flavoured, so the Lorcana room has to work a little harder to feel at home. Print paperandpod.com beside the logo, because the ampersand cannot be typed into a browser.
  • Tuckstead. One word with no i, so the tab tittle has nowhere to go. The signature becomes a joined “ck”, the c’s terminal running into the k’s stem at the tab’s shoulder angle. The tab mark gains a meaning here: it is drawn like a tuck-box flap, so mark and name tell one story, and the kraft packaging leans in harder than under any other name. A coined word has to be taught, so the first-run copy explains it once (“A tuck box is where a deck lives. This is its stead.”).
  • Bring a Deck. A sentence, not a noun, so the voice has to sound like the invitation it is. The wordmark sets the “a” smaller and tucked between the two words, and the stacked lockup (“Bring a / Deck”) becomes the default for square formats. Line names do not follow the brand name in navigation; they stand alone under the wordmark (“Tools”, “Supplies”). The mark carries every size under 30 mm, because three words do not fit a 25 mm label.
  • Seventh Card. The tab mark sits over the “v” as a tittle would, and the lockup stays on one line because “Seventh Card” is two ordinary words. The name refers to the seven-card opening hand that Magic, Pokémon and Lorcana share, so it needs no room-specific or line-specific exception and no imagery rule (D-19). The one thing to watch is the poker read of “seventh card” (seven-card stud); the brand never uses a card-room image, so the read stays faint. First-run copy can explain the name once (“Seven cards in hand. Keep or mulligan.”).
03 Positioning

Riffle Room owns the hour before the shuffle

Positioning

For card players who build their own decks, Riffle Room covers the hour between the decklist and the deck box. Its tools are free and explain what they recommend. What it sells shows its numbers where a buyer can check them: the price per card on paper goods, and MSRP beside our price on sealed product. Pvragon makes it, and the people who make it use it at their own tables.

The promise

We use what we make, and we show you what it costs.

Four checks make the promise testable. A tool explains every recommendation it makes (the Mana Base Builder says why each land is in the list). A Supplies product prints its spec on the pack (sheet count, size, perforation, weight once the supplier confirms it) and its price per card or per sheet on the listing. A paid printable has a free basic version. A sealed listing shows MSRP and market price beside ours, and ours is never above MSRP.

Brand idea

Before the shuffle

Everything Riffle Room makes lives in the hour before a game starts. You finish the brew, fix the mana, print three playtest cards for the ones you have not bought, label the box so the right deck leaves the house, and maybe pick up the new precon. Then you riffle and play. We are not at the table during the game, and the brand should never pretend to be.

It is the internal organising idea, not a public tagline: Moxfield could use it too.

Why the idea belongs to this brand

  • The name is the moment. A riffle is the shuffle that starts every game. “Riffle Room” is the room where you get ready for it. The idea and the name point at the same instant.
  • The physical end of that hour is open. Software already reaches a long way into it: Archidekt covers building, a playtester and a “Buy Cards” button on every deck page (archidekt.com/landing, archidekt.com/faq), and Moxfield holds the list. What no brand in the competitor review has is a physical goods line (playtest paper, labels, dividers, box stock) sold beside free tools, together with a sealed price rule it publishes. That pairing is what Riffle Room can own.
  • It sets the limits. Things that happen during or after the game (life counters, tournament software, match tracking, price speculation after the purchase) are out of scope unless they help with the next “before”. That keeps the brand from turning into an everything-app.
  • It survives a name swap. “Before the shuffle” describes the hour rather than the word, so it holds for all five candidate names.

What Riffle Room is not

  • Not a marketplace. No singles trading, no member-to-member sales. Players already have TCGplayer and their LGS.
  • Not a deck database. Moxfield and Archidekt hold the lists. Our tools read their exports and write back to them.
  • Not a proxy shop. We sell paper and tools. We never sell a printed reproduction of a real card.
  • Not an AI deck generator. Some tools compute things; none is marketed on the word “AI”.
  • Not a price-speculation feed. No “buy now before it spikes”, no investment talk.
  • Not Wizards, and not pretending. No mana symbols, frames or Beleren-like type. Free Magic-facing things carry the Fan Content notice under their own title; sold goods carry a plain non-affiliation line instead.

Sealed is never listed above MSRP

  • Ceiling: we never list sealed product above MSRP. MSRP is set by the publisher, so a scalped market cannot raise it.
  • Where the MSRP comes from (D-18), in this order: the publisher’s published MSRP for that product (label “MSRP, Wizards”); for a box or bundle, the published per-pack MSRP times the pack count (“MSRP, 30 x pack” for a 30-pack Play Booster box); the publisher’s own web store price at release (“Publisher store at release”). If none exists, we do not stock the product. The basis label sits next to the MSRP figure on every listing. The same order applies to Pokémon and Lorcana and is re-checked before those rooms open.
  • Sourcing test: if we cannot source a product below MSRP, we do not stock it. That includes “hot” products.
  • New releases: a per-person limit for the first 30 days after release, stated on the listing before it opens.
  • Every listing shows MSRP, the market price (TCGplayer market or equivalent) and our price side by side, each with the date it was checked.
  • Published sourcing statement: “We buy from distributors, store clearances and private sellers. We never buy new releases at retail to resell them.”

“How this is paid for”, draft

How Riffle Room is paid for

Riffle Room is made by Pvragon, a small company, and the people who build it use these tools at their own tables. The tools are free and need no account. Everything on the list below has been free since it launched and will stay free.

We pay for it by selling things you can hold. Our supplies (playtest paper, label sheets, dividers, deck-box card) show the price per card or per sheet on every listing, so you can compare them with anything else on the shelf. Every paid printable has a free basic version. Paying gets you the designed set or saves you the setup; the free one still does the job.

We also sell sealed product, and never above MSRP. Each listing says where its MSRP comes from: the publisher’s published price, the per-pack price times the packs in a box, or the publisher’s own store price at release. If none of those exists, we don’t stock it. If we can’t buy a product for less than MSRP, we don’t stock it. New releases have a per-person limit for their first 30 days. Every listing shows MSRP, the market price and ours, with the date each was checked. We buy from distributors, store clearances and private sellers. We never buy new releases at retail to resell them.

Some buy links in our tools earn a small commission from the store. The commission never changes what a tool recommends: the Mana Base Builder picks lands for your deck first, then shows the cheapest printing.

If you want to chip in without buying anything, you can become a Regular. Regulars get a discount on supplies and our thanks, and can have their name on the credits page. There is no early access and nothing is timed. Every tool stays free for everyone.

07 Voice

It talks like the player at the next seat

Plain and specific, dry when a joke is true and short, never hyped. It names the actual card and the actual number.

Six principles

  1. Talk like the player at the next seat. Someone who knows the rules cold and will help without being asked twice.
  2. Name the real thing. Use the actual card and the actual number. “Exotic Orchard only makes a color an opponent’s land could make” beats “some lands have conditions”.
  3. Show the working. Say why, and say what we don’t know. “We haven’t confirmed the paper weight with our supplier yet” is on-brand.
  4. Calm commerce. Prices and limits are stated once, plainly. Nothing is urgent.
  5. Dry humour, used sparingly. A joke is allowed when it is true and short. No exclamation-mark enthusiasm. No puns in any message that stops someone doing what they came to do: errors, failed imports and the 404 page.
  6. Fun over power. Never imply a stronger deck is a better one. Talk about the game the pod will have.

Public copy is US English: color, gray, catalog, organize, Dryad Arbor. Prices are in USD, and printables lead with US Letter with A4 beside it.

08.3 Notation

Magic’s colours are letters in squares, never brand colours

One square key per colour, WUBRG then C, with a plain capital in Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono 700 inside an Ink edge. No circles, no mana-symbol shapes: the square and the letter keep clear of Wizards trade dress, and the letter survives 1-bit print and photocopying. Show only the deck’s colours, and never reorder them for emphasis.

WUBRGC

  • Line keys (outline and letter) are the default: every sold product, every 1-bit print, and anywhere colour is uncertain.
  • Hue keys (filled) appear only in the free tools and free printables printed in colour. The fill repeats the letter and never replaces it.

08.7 Imagery

Every illustration and photograph has a named human creator on file, and nothing generated appears anywhere public. Daylight from one side, warm white balance, no coloured gels. 10 Accessibility

AA is the floor on every pairing, in both themes

  1. AA everywhere, as a floor. Every allowed text pairing reaches 4.5:1 at all sizes, and every allowed non-text pairing reaches 3:1. Of 104 allowed text pairings (light, rooms, dark and colour-identity keys), 76 reach AAA (7:1). The lowest are Room Ochre on Ledger (4.82:1) for text and Dark Hairline on Night Raised (3.14:1) for rules. The favicon holds 3:1 on browser chrome too: Proof on a white tab 7.38:1, Dark Proof on Chrome’s dark tabs 7.08:1 and 5.31:1. Recompute with my-lib/runtime/.tmp/261110-v2-palette.py, which exits with an error if any allowed pairing drops below its threshold.
  2. Colour is never the only signal. States carry a word and a glyph; rooms carry a glyph (and a code where allowed); colour identity carries a letter; meters carry a number.
  3. Legible type by design. Atkinson Hyperlegible Next and Mono separate look-alike characters (I l 1, O 0), which matters for set codes and prices. Running text is never under 16 px; print is never under 6 pt.
  4. Keyboard and focus. Every control is reachable by keyboard and shows the Ballpoint focus ring (6.54:1 on Ledger, 8.06:1 on Card).
  5. Reduced motion. Honoured everywhere.
  6. Print legibility. Every printable is designed in black first and proofed as a 1-bit, 300 dpi file and on a home laser printer at 100%. Labels identify a room by glyph and a deck’s colours by letter keys.
  7. Plain language. Error messages say what happened and what to do next. Game terms are explained once for newcomers.
  8. Alt text. Every product photo describes the product and what the hands are doing; tool screenshots describe what the tool shows.

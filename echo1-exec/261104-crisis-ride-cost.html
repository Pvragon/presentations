What does a secured crisis ride cost?

Estimate · Secured crisis rides, July to October 2026 · Echo1 trip logs and Samsara GPS · Updated 2026-11-18

A secured crisis ride costs about $217 to run; half cost under $173. The state rate averages $402, a 46% gross margin if every ride is paid it, but 22% of rides of 30 miles or less lose money, about $15k a month. The $45 contract rate recovers 58% of cost, and 88% of rides lose money under it. Pvragon’s suggested contract price is $145 plus $2.75 a mile after 10 free miles: a 21% gross margin with 22% of rides losing.

A ride costs about $217; the state rate averages $402

From setting out to handing the rider over, a ride takes about 2.2 hours and 99 vehicle miles, 54 of them with the rider aboard. Its share of the time between calls adds 1.3 hours and 39 miles, for 3.5 hours and 138 miles in all. Long rides pull these averages up: the median ride carries the rider 30 miles. The state rate gives a 46% gross margin before dispatch, billing and management, if every ride is paid it.

Paid standby is not in the cost. Mid-day gaps of 3 to 12 hours between a vehicle’s shifts are 12% of shift hours; if drivers are paid through them, a ride costs $13 more. Monthly averages run from $206 to $225 a ride.

Six in ten vehicle miles carry no rider

A ride carries its own drive to the pickup, the wait there, the ride itself and the handover. Empty driving and idle time between two calls is split evenly between the call just finished and the next one, whoever pays for it.

The state rate earns a 46% gross margin on these rides

The state rate is $160 a ride. Above 30 miles it adds $4.80 for every mile from pickup to drop-off, counted from the first mile.

The $45 contract rate sits below cost at every distance, and the suggested price stays above it. The 567 rides over 150 miles (8%, up to 469 miles) are off the chart; they cost about $510 and earn $1,111. The 3,494 rides over 30 miles cost about $315 against $644, and 3% of them lose money. The 3,479 rides of 30 miles or less cost about $118 against the flat $160.

Under the state rate, one short ride in five loses money

The $45 contract rate recovers 58% of a ride’s cost

Applied to these rides, the ODMHSAS and Oceans rate of $45 plus $2.25 a mile over 30 pays about $125 a ride against $217, and 88% of rides lose money. It recovers 38% of cost on short rides and 65% on long ones. Pre-scheduled rides under those contracts lose money as well (measured separately).

A 21% gross margin needs $145 plus $2.75 after 10 miles

$145 plus $2.75 for each mile after the first 10 is Pvragon’s suggested starting point. It is the evenest round price across distance at a 21% gross margin: every distance band pays at least 108% of its cost, and 22% of rides lose money. Gross margin means price minus cost, as a share of price.

  • Keeping $2.25 over 30 needs a $140 flat fee (exactly $137.17) just to break even, and it pays unevenly: short rides pay up to 126% of their cost, rides of 31 to 49 miles 77%.
  • Free miles. Any number of free miles from 0 to about 10 fits cost equally well. Past 10, short rides overpay: at 30 free miles, rides under 20 miles pay 119% of their cost and rides of 31 to 49 miles 75%.
  • Higher margins, with 10 free miles: $160 plus $3.25 earns 31%, and $185 plus $3.75 earns 40%.
  • A threshold price, $140 plus $2.50 for every mile once a ride passes 20, earns 20% and tracks cost more evenly, but a ride one mile past 20 costs $52 more than a ride of 20. Free miles avoid that jump.
  • Dispatch, billing, management and any paid standby are not in the cost, so the margin has to cover them.

Fee, mile rate and free miles trade off at any margin

For a chosen gross margin, any two of flat fee, per-mile rate and mile cutoff fix the third, and each choice sets how many rides lose money and the margin at each distance. Mileage either starts after the free miles or, as in the state rate, counts from the first mile once a ride passes the threshold.

The state rate has applied to every ride since December 2025

  • Rate. Oklahoma’s State Plan amendment OK-25-0018, approved by CMS on March 19, 2026 and effective December 1, 2025, pays $160 a secured crisis ride. Above 30 miles it adds $4.80 for every mile from pickup to drop-off.
  • Threshold. A ride of 30 miles or less earns no mileage.
  • Revenue. Some crisis rides bill other payers or go unpaid, so cash collected can be lower than the state rate.

July to October 2026 gave 6,973 costed secured crisis rides

  • Population. 7,215 secured crisis rides (requested as secured, not pre-scheduled) were completed from July to October 2026. 6,973 are costed. The rest ran in vehicles Samsara no longer reports (55), on days with no GPS shift for the vehicle (186), or had no usable times or loaded distance (1). Uncosted rides run a median 26 loaded miles against 30 for costed ones.
  • Times and miles. Each ride’s drive to the pickup, wait, loaded leg and handover come from the driver’s trip log; hours and miles come from Samsara GPS, in shifts split at idle gaps over 3 hours plus 15 minutes at each end. Drop-off departure was rarely recorded after July, so 68% of handovers use the June and July average of 7 minutes.
  • Shared days. 10% of rides share a shift with another customer’s ride. Each shift’s hours and miles are charged once, every hour and mile to some ride (3,106 shifts, no gap). Charging every empty stretch to the next call instead gives the same $217 a ride.
  • Prices. Drivers cost $28 an hour, payroll taxes 20% of driver pay, and vehicles 72.5 cents a mile.
  • Checks. Echo1’s loaded distances run about 5% above GPS loaded miles, and the state pays on Echo1’s miles; 3% of rides fall on the other side of 30 miles by GPS. None of 32,682 Samsara trips recorded a co-driver. 81% of rides ran in a vehicle Echo1 flags as secured.

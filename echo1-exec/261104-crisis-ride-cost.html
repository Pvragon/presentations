What does a secured crisis ride cost?

Estimate · Secured crisis rides, July to October 2026 · Echo1 trip logs and Samsara GPS · Updated 2026-11-18

A ride costs about $213; the state rate averages $402

Half of rides cost under $166. The state rate gives a 47% gross margin before dispatch, billing and management assuming every ride is paid at the state rate, but 18% of rides of 30 miles or less lose money, about $15k a month. The $45 contract rate recovers 59% of cost, and 87% of rides lose money under it. Pvragon’s suggested contract price is $125 plus $3.00 a mile after 10 free miles: a 20% gross margin with 23% of rides losing.

From setting out to handing the rider over, a ride takes about 2.2 hours and 99 vehicle miles, 53 of them with the rider aboard. Its share of the time between calls adds 1.2 hours and 39 miles, for 3.4 hours and 138 miles in all. Long rides pull these averages up: the median ride carries the rider 30 miles.

Drivers are paid from leaving for the first call until they get home, and not while idle at home. On days with a crisis ride, vehicles sat idle between trips for 9,155 hours: 2,838 at the driver’s home, which is not in the cost, and 6,317 at the office, a facility or elsewhere, which is. Monthly averages run from $206 to $218 a ride.

Six in ten vehicle miles carry no rider

A ride carries its own drive to the pickup, the wait there, the ride itself and the handover. Empty driving and idle time between two calls is split evenly between the call just finished and the next one, whoever pays for it.

The state rate earns a 47% gross margin on these rides

The state rate is $160 a ride. Above 30 miles it adds $4.80 for every mile from pickup to drop-off, counted from the first mile.

The $45 contract rate sits below cost at every distance, and the suggested price stays above it. The 567 rides over 150 miles (8%, up to 469 miles) are off the chart; they cost about $511 and earn $1,111. The 3,489 rides over 30 miles cost about $312 against $644, and 4% of them lose money. The 3,473 rides of 30 miles or less cost about $113 against the flat $160.

Under the state rate, one short ride in five loses money

18% of rides of 30 miles or less cost more than $160, about $15k a month in all. The losing short rides drove a median 113 empty miles to reach the rider and get clear afterwards, against 22 for the short rides that made money. Their rider distances are similar, a median 12 miles against 10.

The $45 contract rate recovers 59% of a ride’s cost

Applied to these rides, the ODMHSAS and Oceans rate of $45 plus $2.25 a mile over 30 pays about $125 a ride against $213, and 87% of rides lose money. It recovers 40% of cost on short rides and 65% on long ones. Pre-scheduled rides under those contracts lose money as well (measured separately).

Five price knobs can move contract rides to solvency

Contract prices turn on five knobs: whether mileage counts from the cutoff or from the first mile once a ride passes it, where that cutoff sits, how the price splits between a flat fee and a mile rate, whether dead legs are billed, and how many riders share the vehicle. At a 20% gross margin, the evenest round price is $125 plus $3.00 a mile after 10 free miles, with 23% of rides losing; $150 plus $3.25 earns 30%. Gross margin is price minus cost, as a share of price. It also has to pay for dispatch, billing and management.

Free miles avoid the price jump that threshold miles create

Free miles charge only the miles past the cutoff; threshold miles, like the state rate, charge every mile once a ride passes it. At about 20%, $135 plus $2.50 for every mile past 20 tracks cost a little more evenly than $125 plus $3.00 after 10, with 22% of rides losing against 23%. The catch is the jump at the threshold: a 21-mile ride pays $52 more than a 20-mile ride.

A 10-mile cutoff tracks cost; at 30, short rides overpay

Any cutoff from 0 to about 10 free miles fits cost equally well. With none at all, $100 plus $3.00 for every mile earns 21%, with 23% of rides losing and every distance band paying at least 107% of its cost. Past 10, short rides start paying for longer ones: at 30 free miles, today’s contract cutoff, rides under 20 miles pay 120% of their cost and rides of 31 to 49 miles 74%. Keeping $2.25 over 30 needs a $135 flat fee to break even, with short rides at up to 127% and 31 to 49 miles at 76%.

Mileage alone pays only on rides over about 56 miles

A ride carries about $92 of cost before its first loaded mile: the drive out, the wait at pickup and the handover. Each loaded mile then adds about $2.14. Billed by the mile alone at $3.78, a ride needs about 56 loaded miles to cover that fixed part; at the state’s $4.80, about 35. A flat fee alone ($213) does the opposite: rides of 30 miles or less pay 189% of their cost and longer ones 68%. Splitting the price as $92 plus $2.14 for every mile breaks even, with the flat fee bringing in 43% of revenue.

Billing dead legs breaks even at $1.54 a mile

Billing dead legs means billing the empty drive to the pickup and back as well as the loaded miles. A ride drives 138 miles for 56 loaded ones, so 62% of its miles are dead legs. Billing all of them breaks even at $1.54 a mile with no flat fee, or $1.22 a mile on top of $45, and earns 20% at $1.93 a mile. At break-even it recovers 82% of cost on rides of 30 miles or less and 106% on longer ones, and 53% of rides still fall below their own cost, because waiting and handover time does not grow with miles. The payer also pays for how far away the nearest vehicle was, which RideCare controls and the payer cannot check without GPS records. On Oceans, billing dead legs turns a $4.0k monthly shortfall into a $13.1k margin (measured separately).

Cost per ride falls steeply as riders share a vehicle

A secured crisis ride always carries one rider, so this knob applies only to shared contract rides. On Oceans rides from June to September, a ride averaged 2.2 riders aboard at its busiest, and the most was 6.

To price a shared ride, divide a single-rider flat fee and mile rate by the divisor for the average riders aboard. $100 plus $3.00 for one rider becomes about $48 plus $1.45 a rider with two aboard and $34 plus $1.03 with three. The divisors for 2 to 4 riders are measured; with only 44 and 6 rides at 5 and 6 or more, those use 82% of the rider count, the fit for 2 to 4. The price at 20% applies the divisor to the single-rider price. A $45 ride covers its cost from about 5 riders aboard. Solo Oceans rides are also longer (25 miles against 14 with three aboard), so the divisors may overstate the saving a little.

Fee, mile rate and free miles trade off at any margin

For a chosen gross margin, any two of flat fee, per-mile rate and mile cutoff fix the third, and each choice sets how many rides lose money and the margin at each distance. Riders sharing a vehicle split its cost evenly, so a contract with fuller vehicles can charge less per rider.

The state rate has applied to every ride since December 2025

  • Rate. Oklahoma’s State Plan amendment OK-25-0018, approved by CMS on March 19, 2026 and effective December 1, 2025, pays $160 a secured crisis ride. Above 30 miles it adds $4.80 for every mile from pickup to drop-off.
  • Threshold. A ride of 30 miles or less earns no mileage.
  • Revenue. Some crisis rides bill other payers or go unpaid, so cash collected can be lower than the state rate.

July to October 2026 gave 6,962 costed secured crisis rides

  • Population. 7,215 secured crisis rides (requested as secured, not pre-scheduled) were completed from July to October 2026. 6,962 are costed. The rest ran in vehicles Samsara no longer reports (55), on days with no GPS shift for the vehicle (197), or had no usable times or loaded distance (1). Uncosted rides run a median 26 loaded miles against 30 for costed ones.
  • Times and miles. Each ride’s drive to the pickup, wait, loaded leg and handover come from the driver’s trip log; hours and miles come from Samsara GPS, counted from the first trip of the day to the last. Idle time at the driver’s home is unpaid and left out; home is the driver’s address in Echo1, matched to the Samsara driver on 77% of trips, else the spot where the vehicle parks overnight (other than the office). Idle time anywhere else, up to 12 hours, is paid. Drop-off departure was rarely recorded after July, so 68% of handovers use the June and July average of 7 minutes.
  • Shared days. 9% of rides share a shift with another customer’s ride. Each shift’s hours and miles are charged once, every hour and mile to some ride (3,757 shifts, no gap). Charging every empty stretch to the next call instead gives the same $213 a ride.
  • Prices. Drivers cost $28 an hour, payroll taxes 20% of driver pay, and vehicles 72.5 cents a mile.
  • Checks. On the 6,668 rides with GPS on the loaded leg, Echo1’s loaded distances run about 2% above GPS loaded miles, and the state pays on Echo1’s miles; 2% of them fall on the other side of 30 miles by GPS. None of 32,695 Samsara trips recorded a co-driver. 81% of rides ran in a vehicle Echo1 flags as secured.

Appendix: a fleet-wide total is within 4% of the ride cost

The fleet-wide total takes every paid driver hour and GPS mile on the 2,384 vehicle-days from July to October when no two riders were aboard at once, divided by the loaded miles of those days’ 6,872 rides (95% of them secured crisis rides).

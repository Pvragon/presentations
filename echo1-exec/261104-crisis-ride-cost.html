What does a secured crisis ride cost?

Estimate · Measured secured crisis rides, January to April 2026 · Updated 2026-11-04

A ride costs about $216; the state rate averages $380

A single-client secured crisis ride takes about 3.4 hours of driver time and 131 vehicle miles, 50 of them with the rider aboard. The state rate on those rides averages about $380, a 43% gross margin before dispatch, billing and management.

A ride’s cost covers the drive out, the pickup wait, the ride itself, and the driving and waiting after dropoff until the vehicle’s next call.

The rider-aboard part is 32% of the cost. A ride of under 10 miles still costs about $112, so the flat fee has to carry most of the price.

The state rate earns a 43% gross margin on these rides

Above 30 miles every mile pays $4.80, and the 2,180 longer rides cost about $326 against $633. 4% of them lose money. At 30 miles or less the rate is a flat $160, and the 2,512 short rides cost about $120.

Under the state rate, one short ride in five loses money

21% of rides of 30 miles or less cost more than $160. The losing short rides drove a median 103 empty miles to reach the rider and get clear afterwards, against 21 for the short rides that made money. Their rider distances are similar, a median 12 miles against 10.

The $45 contract rate recovers 54% of a ride’s cost

Applied to these rides, the ODMHSAS and Oceans rate of $45 plus $2.25 a mile over 30 pays about $117 a ride against $216, and 90% of rides lose money. It recovers 37% of cost on short rides and 61% on long ones. Those contracts’ own rides are measured on the contract-rate page.

At $2.25 over 30, break-even needs a lopsided $145 fee

Keeping $2.25 a mile over 30, the flat fee has to rise to $145 (exactly $144.19) to cover total cost. Short rides then pay up to 129% of their cost, and rides of 31 to 49 miles recover 80%. The price balances only at this distance mix; a contract with more rides of 31 to 49 miles would lose money.

With 10 free miles, every distance recovers 88% to 108%

A $110 flat fee plus $2.50 for each mile after the first 10 also breaks even, and keeps every distance band close to its cost.

The state rate has applied to every ride since December 2025

  • Rate. Oklahoma’s State Plan amendment OK-25-0018, approved by CMS on March 19, 2026 and effective December 1, 2025, pays $160 a secured crisis ride. Above 30 miles it adds $4.80 for every mile from pickup to dropoff.
  • Threshold. A ride of 30 miles or less earns no mileage. V1 billed mileage on those rides anyway, so V1’s charges run above the rate this page uses.
  • Revenue. The rate is what the state schedule pays. Some crisis rides bill other payers or go unpaid, so cash collected can be lower.

The costs come from 4,692 measured secured crisis rides

  • Population. 7,206 crisis rides were completed from January to April 2026. The 4,692 costed rides were requested as secured, rode alone, and had Samsara GPS and clean stop times. The rest lacked GPS or route times (1,662), shared the vehicle (321), were not requested as secured (168), or had bad stop times (363).
  • Distance mix. Costed rides run a median 25 loaded miles, against 24 for all secured crisis rides; 54% run 30 miles or less, against 57%.
  • Checks. V1 records about 5% more loaded miles than GPS, and the state pays on V1’s miles. Samsara shifts average 7.9 hours per V1 driver-day. None of 50,791 trips recorded a co-driver.
  • Standby. Mid-day gaps of 3 to 12 hours, 7% of shift hours, are priced as paid standby. If they are unpaid, the cost falls by $8.
  • Left out. Dispatch, billing, management, office and insurance beyond the IRS mileage rate, which the margin has to cover.

FY27 contract: crisis ride paperwork

Each crisis ride needs an LMHP statement or court order.

Version 3.27.0Updated 2026-11-19

Summary

  • The paperwork required is an LMHP statement or a court order. Nothing else counts, and it has been the rule since October 1, 2025.
  • Eval rides are a gray zone. ODMHSAS doesn’t route them to us. Their only paperwork is the officer or third-party statement, and the contract doesn’t specifically allow for that. This may be worth clarifying up front in negotiations.
  • What’s new in FY27 is Section 6. The paperwork becomes an audited eligibility record that must be contemporaneous.
  • Everything points to having paperwork before the ride is done. The SOW’s wording and ODMHSAS’s own decision tree both put the document before the ride. No clause ties it to billing.
  • About 75% of crisis rides have a document on file. The other roughly 25% of crisis revenue represents ongoing compliance risk.
  • An SSN is the practical route to 53AC payment. DMH’s SSN requirement is legally questionable. Emergency Medicaid may be a path for riders who can’t get one.
  • FYI, the arrival requirement is 90 minutes, never over 120. 180 minutes is an approval trigger, not a three-hour window.
  • Two payers, one rulebook for the ride. SoonerCare and 53AC (ODMHSAS’s money) bill differently, but the SOW’s rules govern every ride.
  • The contract cap is 53AC money only and doesn’t include SoonerCare billing. FY26 records support that with reasonable confidence.
  • This year adds that we must self-report a known pattern of improper rides.
  • Legal eligibility needs no checks before the ride. There’s no citizenship, residency or income test. Coverage only decides who pays.
  • The risk escalates. It runs from repayment, to payment cuts, to suspension, to losing the contract.

  • The SOW’s paperwork, response-time, no-stops and training rules apply to every ride, SoonerCare and 53AC alike.
  • Both payers are billed through Medicaid’s claims system (MMIS), with code 53AC marking riders without Medicaid (SOW p. 3).

  • Managed-care plans may add a third rule set. SoonerSelect plans such as Humana and Aetna are asking for the LMHP statement with claims (David Roberts, 11/17). What their plan contracts require is unknown.

Who is eligible

A rider is eligible for this service when they hold a 43A legal status: “emergency detention status, court commitment status or court ordered for evaluation” (SOW p. 1).

  • The paperwork is an “LMHP Statement (Emergency Detention)” or a “Court Order” (SOW p. 2). The SOW names no other document.
  • It is also the eligibility record Section 6.2 requires, which puts it under Section 6’s audit remedies. We keep a record or copy for every ride, whoever pays (SOW p. 2).
  • SoonerCare’s own test is looser. OHCA pays on a reasonable belief of crisis, medical necessity and the nearest appropriate facility, and names no document (317:30-5-349). The SOW’s status and paperwork still govern how every ride is run.
43A document Stage In the SOW?
Peace officer statement (§ 5-207(C)) Protective custody, before any exam No
Third-party statement (§ 5-207(C)) Protective custody, before any exam No
LMHP statement (§ 5-208(A)(3)) Exam finds the person needs treatment; emergency detention begins Yes
Court order (§ 5-208 and later) Detention or commitment ordered by a judge Yes

A hospital emergency detention order (EOD) is the facility’s order carrying out the LMHP’s finding; 43A does not name it. Minors follow a separate sequence (§ 5-506): emergency admission, an LMHP evaluation, then a court prehearing detention order.

Riders also need coverage under one program or the other for us to be paid: SoonerCare, or DMH (code 53AC) for riders with no other coverage. DMH enrollment currently requires an SSN, so getting one is very helpful. The legal eligibility rules (citizenship, residency, income and coverage) are in the appendix.

  • Record. The same page reads: “Contractor will maintain a record and/or copy of the appropriate documentation for transport and follow and abide by HIPAA, privacy, and record keeping laws.”
  • Contemporaneous. Att. A §6.2 requires “accurate, complete, and contemporaneous records” of eligibility, provided to ODMHSAS on request. Neither the contract nor the Services Manual defines the word or sets a number of days; in ordinary legal usage it means made at or near the time of the event.
  • The SOW is not in the signed packet. We assume the contract binds ODMHSAS’s posted FY27 Secure Mental Health Transportation SOW, dated 2026-10-23, by reference (Att. A §2.1). If so, ODMHSAS can change it without an amendment, because the contract binds the most recent posting.

  • The paperwork rule dates from October 1, 2025. “Only transport with” an LMHP statement or court order appears in the same words in the FY26 contract (p. 25). FY27 adds the Section 6 duty to keep that document as a contemporaneous eligibility record, which points further toward having it at transport. How binding to treat the rule is leadership’s call.
  • Arrival time since FY22. Every SOW since FY22 sets a 90-minute target and a 120-minute ceiling. Until October 2025, missing the 90-minute target needed ODMHSAS approval; since then, approval is required only beyond 180 minutes. Arriving between 120 and 180 minutes still breaks the 120-minute ceiling, and the contract sets no three-hour window.

When the paperwork is needed

  • The rule’s verb is transport: “Contractor will only transport with appropriate documentation” (SOW p. 2).
  • Eligibility is a status held at pickup: people “placed in emergency detention status, court commitment status or court ordered for evaluation” (SOW p. 1).
  • One trip type says it outright: facility-to-facility only if the member “has been assessed as meeting criteria for emergency detention prior to transport” (SOW p. 2; OHCA 317:30-5-349 says the same).
  • The no-stops rule assumes the status during the ride: “Due to the fact secure transports are for those in Emergency Detention status or under a Court Order for evaluation or treatment” (SOW p. 3).
  • If a rider runs, the driver tells police the “status of individual (emergency detention, court committed to inpatient treatment, etc)” (SOW p. 2), which assumes we know the status during the ride.
  • ODMHSAS’s own decision tree calls us only after an emergency-detention finding. On every path that ends in a request to us, a facility or evaluator has first found that the person meets emergency detention criteria (see the appendix). The LMHP statement records that finding.
  • No clause ties the document to billing. The billing terms cover MMIS claims, code 53AC and one-way rides with the rider in the vehicle; none asks for the document with a claim. Managed-care plans asking for it with claims is payer practice (David Roberts, 11/17).

What the contract leaves open

  • Who collects the paperwork. The contract does not assign it to drivers, dispatch or the facility.
  • How it reaches us. Fax, upload, paper and photo all fit “record and/or copy”; the contract’s definition of a record includes photographs (Att. B §10.1).
  • When our copy arrives, provided the document existed at transport and the record is contemporaneous.
  • An SSN. Nothing in the contract requires one to transport. An SSN is required to enroll a rider in DMH coverage, which is 53AC coverage for riders with no insurance; DMH’s other enrollment rules are unconfirmed.
  • Prior authorization. OHCA requires none except that out-of-state trips may need one (317:30-5-351). Whether ODMHSAS’s Prior Authorization Manual applies to code 53AC is unconfirmed.

  • All five options satisfy the transport condition when the document existed at transport. Late copies (option E, and B or C when the copy lags) put the Section 6.2 contemporaneous-record duty at risk, and a ride that had no document at transport stays out of compliance whenever a copy arrives.
  • Option B is Toni’s proposal from 11/17: facilities fax to a RideCare line, and dispatch matches the fax to the trip and uploads it. Destroying the paper afterwards is fine once the uploaded copy is kept as the record, for 7 years after the contract ends (Att. B §10.2).

Eval and out-of-scope rides

Document Named in the contract?
LMHP statement Yes
Court order Yes
Hospital emergency detention order (EOD) No; records the LMHP’s finding
Peace officer or third-party statement No; named in Title 43A

Who can sign an LMHP statement: an LMHP as defined in 43A § 1-103(11). The LBHP title alone does not qualify; the Services Manual says “An LBHP is not equivalent to an LMHP in 43A” (FY27 Services Manual p. 8). Most licenses behind an LBHP title are also LMHP licenses: psychiatrist or other physician, psychologist, LPC, LCSW, LMFT, licensed behavioral practitioner, psychiatric APRN and PA. An alcohol and drug counselor without the MH designation, or a licensure candidate, is not an LMHP.

Eval rides

An eval ride carries a person from protective custody to their first evaluation, before any LMHP statement can exist. In our reading, pending counsel:

SoonerCare rider No Medicaid (53AC)
Inside the SOW’s population? No No
Payer’s rule Names protective custody; appears billable Likely not billable
Exposure Counts toward a pattern we must self-report (Att. A §6.3) Payment recoverable; same reporting risk
  • ODMHSAS does not route these rides to us. Its decision tree has law enforcement take the person to an evaluation location, or brings the evaluation to them by telehealth or a mobile crisis team, and requests our transport only after the evaluation.
  • 43A still assigns some to contracted transport (§ 1-110(B)), including people placed in protective custody at an ER.
  • The paperwork is the officer or third-party statement (§ 5-207(C)), if one was written. The SOW does not name it; whether Att. A §6.5’s duty to apply “all eligibility exceptions” brings it in is for counsel.
  • An ER pickup after a physician’s exam is not an eval ride. A physician counts as an LMHP, so the usual paperwork applies.
  • Minors face a similar gap on rides to emergency admission, before their LMHP evaluation (§ 5-506).

Out-of-scope rides

The SOW says the contractor “will only complete the following types of transports”: to hospitals and behavioral health facilities ODMHSAS authorizes; facility-to-facility, if the member “has been assessed as meeting criteria for emergency detention prior to transport”; and court-ordered transport “for the purpose of evaluation or crisis stabilization or admittance to a treatment bed” (SOW p. 2). These rides fall outside it:

  • Facility-to-facility transfers without a prior emergency-detention assessment, such as voluntary or step-down transfers. FY25 SOWs allowed “occasional facility-to-facility transportation for step down care”; the FY26 and FY27 secure SOWs dropped it, and the FY27 contract has no other line for it.
  • Rides to anywhere other than an authorized hospital or behavioral health facility, such as a discharge home or to a shelter.
  • Court-ordered rides for other purposes, such as to a court hearing.
  • Riders with no 43A status and no court order, such as a voluntary patient.

Compliance risk of riding without paperwork

In our reading, a ride with no LMHP statement or court order at pickup is outside the contract’s terms, except possibly an eval ride, and leaves no contemporaneous eligibility record. ODMHSAS’s responses, from least to most severe:

Level What ODMHSAS can do Where
1 Recover improper payments after a review; 60 days to repay Att. A §6.4, 28
2 Cut later payments after a random eligibility or service check Att. A §15.6, 26
3 Suspend payments Att. A §15.9, 6.4
4 Treat it as a material breach and end the contract Att. A §6.4
  • Ending the contract normally takes notice and 30 days to cure (Att. B §18.1). Immediate termination is allowed for safety, privacy and similar failures, and for a material breach the state finds detrimental to it (Att. B §18.2). Either side may also end it on 30 days’ notice without cause (Att. A §15.5).
  • We must report problems ourselves. Section 6.3 requires us to notify ODMHSAS of a known material pattern of ineligible or improperly billed services. A known practice of riding without paperwork could become one.

Financial risk of riding without paperwork

  • Per ride: ODMHSAS can recover a 53AC payment. On a SoonerCare ride, OHCA’s rule names no document, so whether ODMHSAS can recover it under this contract is unclear.
  • Overall: 72 to 78% of crisis rides have a document on file, so roughly a quarter do not. If ODMHSAS clawed back payment on all of those, about 25% of crisis-ride revenue since October 1 would be at risk, less any of it paid by SoonerCare that ODMHSAS cannot reach.
  • Suspension: Section 15.9 lets ODMHSAS suspend payment for any non-compliance, with no stated limit; Section 6.4 covers the affected payments. Whether a suspension would reach SoonerCare claims is not stated.
  • Termination ends ODMHSAS and SoonerCare secure-transport billing, because OHCA pays only ODMHSAS-contracted providers (317:30-5-350).
  • Declining at pickup costs the driver’s time and the empty miles. The contract bills only rides with the person in the vehicle; whether a declined ride can still bill a trip charge is unconfirmed.

Decisions for RideCare

  1. Who collects the paperwork and through which channel (options A to E, alone or combined).
  2. Whether to dispatch before the document is confirmed, given the response requirement (90 minutes, not over 120).
  3. What happens when no document exists at pickup: decline, ride under another payer, or ride and accept the risk.
  4. How to handle eval and minor rides once counsel has read the statute against the contract.
  5. The written policies the contract requires: eligibility (6.1), ethical billing and quarterly review (15.7-15.8).
  6. Section 6.3: whether rides with no document since October 1 are a reportable pattern, with counsel.

Appendix

Legal eligibility

Section 6 requires following federal and Oklahoma law on citizenship, immigration status and eligibility for public benefits, and our written policy must name those requirements for each service (Att. A §6.1). The contract adds none of its own, and Section 6.5 bars applying any the law does not authorize.

Criterion Rule for crisis transport What we verify and document
Citizenship and immigration status Exempt Nothing
Residency No rule Nothing
Income No rule for emergency services Nothing
Other coverage Bill it before 53AC (Att. A §30) Coverage on the date of service
DMH enrollment Required for 53AC Enrollment, including SSN
  • We do not need to verify citizenship. Federal law and Oklahoma’s verification statute (Title 56, §71) exempt care for “an emergency medical condition,” meaning symptoms severe enough that without immediate attention the patient’s health is placed “in serious jeopardy.” The Attorney General’s July 2025 order also states that the law does not preclude aliens from receiving “ambulance, transportation (including paratransit)” and similar services.
  • Residency and income carry no rule. The SOW and the Services Manual set no residency rule, and the eligibility matrix sets no income test for emergency services.
  • Coverage decides the payer. A rider with SoonerCare or other coverage is billed there. A rider with none is a 53AC ride, which needs DMH enrollment.
  • DMH’s SSN requirement may not hold up. Medicaid can require an SSN because a federal statute says so, with exceptions for people who cannot get one. No federal statute requires an SSN for state-funded DMH coverage, and the federal Privacy Act bars a state agency from denying a benefit because a person will not disclose an SSN. In our reading, the rule is ODMHSAS’s to resolve; until then, collecting an SSN is the practical route to payment.
  • None of these checks has to happen before the ride. Coverage and enrollment are billing conditions: they decide who pays and whether we are paid.
  • Customer Data Core (CDC) records: the contract makes them a condition of ODMHSAS and Medicaid payment (Att. A §21, §26.2). RideCare has never filed one for transport, and we assume they do not apply.

ODMHSAS’s transportation decision tree

ODMHSAS posts a “Comprehensive Crisis Response Transportation Decision Tree” on its transportation page (decision tree image, dated 2026-09-25). It names no documents.

Its paths, as drawn (CCBHC is a certified community behavioral health clinic):

Path Start Steps Outcome
F1 Facility Already assessed as meeting emergency detention criteria; closest suitable facility confirmed in the request Facility requests our transport
F2 Facility Not yet assessed; evaluation (telehealth through a CCBHC) finds criteria met Same as F1
F3 Facility Evaluation finds criteria not met No secure transport; voluntary admission rides are the receiving facility’s or the person’s
L1 Law enforcement Telehealth evaluation on an ODMHSAS iPad; de-escalated, or criteria not met and no protective custody No transport
L2 Law enforcement Telehealth evaluation finds criteria met Distance step

In our reading:

  • Every request to us follows an emergency-detention finding (F1, F2, D1). The evaluator who makes the finding also requests the ride, so the LMHP statement should already exist and sit with the requester.
  • The ride to an evaluation is the officer’s (L3), or the evaluation comes to the person (L1, L2, L4). No path sends an eval ride to us.
  • Voluntary patients are out (F3), matching the SOW.
  • Law-enforcement cases under 30 miles go to the officer (D2). The FY27 SOW sets no distance limit, so this is ODMHSAS’s routing. Facility requests (F1, F2) have no distance split.
  • The tree is guidance from ODMHSAS’s public page; the documents the contract binds sit on its provider site.

Emergency Medicaid for riders without an SSN

Some riders cannot get an SSN, and since October 1, 2026 many lawfully present noncitizens, including refugees and asylees, no longer qualify for full SoonerCare. Both groups may still qualify for emergency Medicaid, which would move their rides from 53AC to SoonerCare, outside the 53AC cap and without DMH enrollment or an SSN.

  • The ID comes without an SSN. Medicaid exempts anyone “not eligible to receive an SSN” from its SSN rule and gives them a Medicaid ID number instead (42 C.F.R. § 435.910(h)).
  • The coverage is limited to emergencies. Oklahoma’s emergency Medicaid covers “care and services necessary for the treatment of an emergency medical condition” for a noncitizen who otherwise meets SoonerCare’s requirements, including income and Oklahoma residency (OAC 317:35-5-25).
  • Approval is case by case, on paper. The provider submits Form MS-MA-5, “Notification of Needed Medical Services,” to the county OKDHS office. OHCA’s medical staff then review the claim and medical records after the service to confirm it was an emergency (OAC 317:30-3-32). The rule names no electronic route.
  • The claim may still go through Waystar. In our reading, an approval creates an emergency-only eligibility period in Medicaid’s claims system, which Waystar submits to. The rule does not say so.
  • The receiving hospital may already file. Hospitals routinely file emergency Medicaid for the same episode. If that approval covers the ride date, our claim might match the same member ID, which we would collect from the facility as we do the paperwork. This is unconfirmed.

Whether OHCA’s review treats secure transport as an emergency service is unconfirmed, as is whether one form covers a whole episode or each ride. An Echo1 count of uninsured crisis rides without an SSN would show whether the volume justifies the work.

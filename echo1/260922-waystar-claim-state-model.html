Waystar claim states, from 837 upload to the five 835 states

The lifecycle of a claim through Waystar, and the five states a claim can be in once at least one 835 loop exists: net negative, denied, partially paid, paid in full, net overpaid.

Waystar docs captured 2026-08-08Measured 2026-09-22v0.1.0

1. The lifecycle

Terms used in the lifecycle

First-level and second-level edits are Waystar’s two rounds of checks before a claim goes anywhere (Support Centre article What are the clearinghouse first and second level edits, revised 2026-01-06). First-level edits are name matching, payer enrollment and account-specific rules; they run “before the claim is processed and the claim has not been billed to the client.” Second-level edits are the HIPAA structure checks, business validations such as NPI, diagnosis and procedure codes valid for the date of service, optional coding-compliance packages (CCI, LCD, NCD), and payer- or intermediary-specific rules; they count toward monthly billing. A claim stops at the first second-level edit it fails, and a corrected resubmission can fail a later one.

Event codes in square brackets are Waystar’s Claims Disposition Report codes (the full list is in §3.2). Waystar publishes the list without explaining its grammar; the pattern in the list is that the letters before the hyphen name the event and the letter after it names who did it: -T for Waystar’s own processing, -P the payer, -I an intermediary, -R a repricer, and -Z on the one first-edits rejection. So A-T is passed all edits at Waystar, FP-T is sent to the payer by Waystar, R-P is rejected by the payer, D-P denied by the payer, P-P paid by the payer. The codes reach a customer only through the CDR file, which is not provisioned for our account; the same events appear as text in each claim’s Waystar history.

277 category codes (A1, A3, P0, F2 and so on) are the X12 claim-status categories a payer returns to a status inquiry, listed with Waystar’s labels in §3.3.

Frequency code is the X12 claim frequency type code in Loop 2300 CLM05-3 of the 837, the third part of the composite that also carries place of service. It tells the payer what kind of submission this is: 1 an original claim, 7 a replacement of a prior claim (the payer re-adjudicates using this version), 8 a void of a prior claim (the payer reverses it). A 7 or an 8 must also carry the payer’s control number for the original adjudication, the ICN, in REF*F8; without it the payer answers original claim not found. Medicare accepts neither on professional claims, and takes a corrected claim as a 1 with a claim note instead.

ICN is the payer’s own claim identifier, assigned when it adjudicates. It arrives in the 835 as CLP07, one per adjudication, so a claim that was corrected and re-adjudicated has more than one.

Net is the sum of every 835 claim payment amount (CLP04) for the claim across all its loops; a reversal loop carries a negative amount, so it subtracts. Billed is the claim’s total charge (CLP03) on a non-reversal loop.

Three points in the lifecycle are easy to get wrong:

  1. Rejection and denial are different exits. A rejection (Waystar’s edits, or the payer’s front end) happens before adjudication: no ICN, no 835, nothing to reverse. Waystar’s 277 vocabulary keeps them apart (A3/A6/A7 are Rejected, F2 is Denied), and so does the CDR: R-P CLAIM REJECTED BY PAYER versus D-P CLAIM DENIED BY PAYER. A rejected claim is resubmitted as an original (frequency 1); a denied one is corrected with frequency 7 or 8 and the payer’s ICN.
  2. “Received by Payer” is where every adjudicated claim lives. The portal’s Status column (the spine’s Status) is a transport state and never advances past it. Measured across all 64,944 spine rows the column takes five values only: Received by Payer (64,786), Rejected by Payer (66), Rejected by Waystar (59), In Progress (29), Rejected - Name Matching Required (4). Paid, denied and partial are not portal statuses. They exist only on the 835 side.
  3. The state is the net of every loop, and it is never final. One claim can carry several ICNs (each resubmission gets a new one) and several loops per ICN (a reversal is a second loop under the same ICN with CLP02 = 22 and negative money). A later 835 can move a claim between states at any time.

2. The five states

Rule. For a claim with at least one 835 CLP loop, let net = Σ CLP04 over every loop (reversals carry negative CLP04, so they subtract) and billed = CLP03 of the adjudication (the largest non-reversal CLP03 when they differ). Then the claim is in exactly one of:

# state test what the money is what it means first action
1 Net negative net < 0 net (cash taken back beyond what these files show arriving) a reversal in our files exceeds the payments in our files: either the original payment predates our 835 coverage, or a recoupment landed on this loop find the missing payment (payer RA in data/non-waystar-eobs/, or an earlier era) before treating it as owed
2 Denied net = 0 billed the payer adjudicated and paid nothing read the CAS codes; correct + frequency 7, void + 8, or appeal
3 Partially paid 0 < net < billed billed − net (the shortfall) at least one line paid less than billed work at line grain (§4): which lines, which group code
4 Paid in full net = billed net every line paid at billed nothing
5 Net overpaid net > billed net − billed (the excess we hold) usually a duplicate payment not yet reversed; a refund liability expect a CLP02 = 22 reversal; if none comes, it is ours to return

At the claim level paid in full means every service line paid at its billed amount (Jaime, 2026-09-22). Adjustment codes do not change the bucket: a claim paid at the contracted rate with a CO-45 write-down is partially paid, because by the 2026-08-09 ruling no adjustment code settles a claim.

Why five. States 2–4 cover every net between zero and billed inclusive. Net can also fall below zero or above billed, and both happen:

Measured 2026-09-22 across 920 ERA files and 107,687 claim loops:

state claims money money is
net negative 13 −4,302.20 Σ net
denied 4,305 1,653,416.68 Σ billed
partially paid 10,568 1,841,792.81 Σ (billed − net)
paid in full 46,460 22,876,172.86 Σ net
net overpaid 70 31,071.95 Σ (net − billed)

61,416 claims carry at least one loop and a stated charge. All 13 net-negative claims are legacy-timestamp account numbers whose original payment predates the earliest 835 on file (2025-02-26): a gap in our records, not a payer position.

Waystar names four in the Denial Rate Report article and lists the full set it maps in the Remittance Crosswalk (row 111): 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 10, 13, 15, 16, 17, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 25, 27. The ones that occur in our files, measured:

Waystar’s own warning about 4, verbatim: “The claim status is set to 4 ONLY when the entire claim is denied. If individual line items (procedures) are denied, but the entire claim has not been denied, then the remit will not appear on this report.” The measurement says the same thing from the other side: 13,284 primary adjudications paid zero under status 1 against 1,278 under status 4. Denied is read from the money, never from CLP02.

19, 20, 21, 23 (processed and forwarded to another payer) and 25, 27 are in Waystar’s map but do not occur in our files.

4. Line grain: why a partial is worked per line

Adjudication is per service line. Each ICN restates the full line set with its own money, and the claim-level CLP04 is the sum. Waystar’s Denial Management product works at this grain:

  • Fully Paid (Denial Management guide, auto-close rules): “The paid amount equals the billed amount, and no adjustment codes were returned.”
  • Partially Paid (same): “The paid amount is less than the billed amount and no adjustment codes were returned.”
  • Workable vs informational. Every CAS adjustment is tagged workable or informational by the customer’s own tags, categories and auto-close rules. A remit is denied in Denial Management “if there were any ‘Workable’ adjustment lines on the remits … BOTH claim and line level … denied in full or partially.” So Waystar’s product definition of a denial is any workable adjustment, not paid zero.
  • Denied amount has three calculations and the customer picks one: billed − paid, allowed − paid, or the sum of workable adjustments. Waystar’s worked example gives 400, 100 and a code-driven figure for the same remit. This document’s state table uses billed − paid.

Adjustment group codes, as the 835 carries them in CAS01:

group name collectable as stated?
PR patient responsibility from the patient
CO contractual obligation no, but by the 2026-08-09 ruling every CO-45 here is owed, not written down
PI payer-initiated reduction appealable
OA other adjustment depends on the reason code; OA-18 (duplicate) splits two ways

A partially paid claim is therefore a list of lines, each with its paid, its CAS group and reason, and its remark codes (LQ*HE).

5. The loop back: corrected claims

Covered in full in waystar-corrected-claim-workflow.md. The parts the flowchart depends on:

  • A rejection is resubmitted as an original. Frequency 1, no REF*F8. Auto-Archive (if enabled) will fold the old rejected instance onto the new one by CLM01.
  • A denial or partial is corrected with a reference to the adjudication. Frequency 7 (replacement) or 8 (void) in CLM05-3, and the payer’s ICN in REF*F8. The payer answers with a new ICN for the correction and a CLP02 = 22 reversal under the original ICN, and the claim re-nets. Measured end to end on the two Echo1 corrections of 2026-09-02.
  • Medicare refuses 7 and 8 on professional claims; the correction goes as frequency 1 with a claim note.
  • A net-overpaid claim needs no action from us to correct; the payer’s reversal is the expected next loop. If it never comes, the excess is a refund we owe.

Every corrected claim is a new 837 through the same SFTP upload as an original. The answer arrives on the same 835 feed: a new ICN for the correction and a status-22 reversal under the original ICN, and the claim re-nets.

7. Deciding the state: the procedure

For one claim (one account number as the payer echoes it in CLP01):

  1. Collect every CLP loop across every ERA file whose CLP01 is this claim. Include reversals.
  2. If there are none: the claim is not adjudicated. Separate rejected (a rejection event in Waystar’s history, or portal Status Rejected by …) from awaiting (portal Status Received by Payer, no loop yet). Neither is in the five states.
  3. net = Σ CLP04. billed = max CLP03 over loops with CLP02 ≠ 22.
  4. Bucket by the table in §2.
  5. Record the evidence with the verdict: the file, the ICN (CLP07), the CLP02, and for states 1, 2, 3 the CAS lines.
  6. Treat the state as current, not final. The next ERA file can change it.

8. Not covered

  • Whether the Claims Disposition Report, 277U or Claim Monitoring can be provisioned for our account. All are documented; none is delivered to us today.
  • Payer-portal remittances outside Waystar (data/non-waystar-eobs/). They overlap the ERA corpus for SoonerCare and would be the second source for state 1.
  • Line-level state. The counts above use the claim net; the per-line test (every line paid at billed) is the definition but is not yet computed.
  • Institutional claims (837I). Everything measured is professional.

Payer configuration: requirements and proposed design

The capabilities any payer and tenant configuration must have to bill every tenant's contracts in any US state, followed by one proposed design that meets them: one dated schedule per payer, rate lines for every priced term, billing identities chosen at the payer level, and a stamp on every claim.

Proposed design, draftDesign v0.8.4-draftEcho1 origin/main aa0d1650dUpdated 2026-10-03

Requirements

These are the capabilities a payer and tenant configuration must have, whatever tables implement them. They are written for any tenant billing any payer in any US state: state Medicaid, managed care plans, transport brokers, counties, hospitals and state agencies on contract. Every requirement comes from a signed contract, a state billing manual or a federal rule. Echo1 decides how to implement them; the proposed design is one way to meet them.

Each requirement has one of two levels:

  • Now: a current RideCare payer, or a payer type a new tenant will certainly bring, needs it.
  • Room: no current payer needs it yet, but adding it later must be an addition (a new option value, column or table), never a restructure. Until it is built, configuration that needs it is refused with a named reason.

1. Foundations

# The system must Level Example
R1 Let a tenant set up any payer through named options and configured rows. Adding a payer never needs a code change; a new behaviour is a reviewed new option that every tenant can use Now 26 hypothetical payers across many states walk through without per-payer code
R2 Isolate each tenant’s identities, payers, prices and claims from every other tenant, enforced in the database Now two transport companies on one platform
R3 Let rules common to many payers be kept once as a shared, dated template, and let a payer override a single setting without copying the rest Now Colorado’s state 837 rules, used by every Colorado payer
R4 Date every price and every billing term. Prices and terms apply on the transport’s service date; electronic routing applies on the date the file is sent Now Oklahoma mileage rose from $2.85 to $4.80 on 2025-12-01; a payer changes its electronic id, then an older claim is rebilled
R5 Change configuration only by adding a dated version. Versions follow each other without gaps, except after a deliberate deactivation. A version a billed claim used cannot be edited or back-dated under Now a rate correction must not silently reprice claims already sent
R6 Give exactly one price and one set of claim terms for every transport. Two settings that could decide the same money are refused when saved. Ambiguity is never settled by row order or by a person choosing at billing time Now Larned’s “$85 under 50 miles” and “$2.85 a mile at 50 and over” can never both apply
R7 Stop with a named reason when anything needed is missing: a measurement, an index value, a setting, an address, a version in force. A missing value never prices as zero or as “no limit” Now a fuel surcharge with no fuel price for the week
R8 Record on every claim the exact configuration versions, billing identity and address it was built from, and how each charge was derived per transport. A replacement or void years later reproduces the original exactly Now an audit asks why a 2026 claim billed 13 miles
R9 Price only from the payer and measured facts of the transport: distance, wait, stops, staff, where it went, when it was cancelled. Who pays is decided before pricing, never inside a price rule Now TANF and crisis transports are separate payers, not price conditions

2. Tenant and billing identity

# The system must Level Example
R10 Let a tenant bill under several legal identities, each with legal name, NPI, tax ID, taxonomy, address and contact Now RideCare bills as OK RIDE CARE and as David Roberts Consulting
R11 Bill each payer under one identity: the one its configuration names, or the tenant’s dated default. Changing the default reaches every payer that follows it from a date, without editing those payers Now a company with one identity never sets one per payer
R12 Date address and contact changes on the identity without touching any payer. A new NPI or tax ID is a new identity Now the company moves offices
R13 Send a payer-issued enrolment number, taxonomy override or contract code only with the identity it was issued to Now Colorado’s provider number 11638 belongs to DRC alone
R14 Support sites separately enrolled with one payer as separate payer records that roll up for reporting and may share an electronic payer id Now “SoonerCare, Tulsa site” and “SoonerCare, OKC site”
R15 Date each transport in the timezone where it happens, whatever the server’s or viewer’s zone. Period boundaries such as months and invoice weeks use a configured timezone Now a 23:20 CDT pickup belongs to that day, though it is 04:20 UTC the next day
R16 Hold tenant settings: business days and holidays, claim number prefix, clearinghouse accounts Now an invoice dated the last business day of the month

# The system must Level Example

R24 Price expenses at cost, attendant time and no-shows as terms Room Larned’s preauthorised travel expenses
R25 Give each term its own included allowance before charging starts Now Oklahoma’s first 30 miles; Larned’s first 30 minutes of wait; none in Colorado

R29 Apply a term only when a measured condition holds Now $1.00 a mile when two staff are aboard; a surcharge when fuel tops $5.00

R31 Guarantee a minimum per period, overall or per referring facility Room a county guaranteeing each facility $2,000 a month
R32 Adjust a rate by a dated published index, with a step rule Now Larned: +$0.50 a mile above $5.00 a gallon, +$0.04 per further $1.00
R33 Cap a payer by dollars, priced trips or transports per month, year or contract term, from a chosen start Now Larned $104,000 a year; Chickasaw 65 transports a month

5. Distance and rounding

# The system must Level Example
R35 Set the distance rule per priced term: one way or round trip; between the client’s own pickup and dropoff, or from a fixed reference location; or the vehicle’s driven path shared among riders Now Larned’s first passenger is priced round trip from Larned, further passengers one way between their own stops
R36 Measure distance from the map now, and leave room for odometer readings with a fallback rule Room Arizona requires loaded miles from the odometer
R37 Round each quantity per term: up, down or half up; whole or tenths; a precision that changes above a threshold Now Medicare rounds up to tenths under 100 miles and to whole miles at 100 and over
R38 Price time in any unit size Now per hour; Arizona per 30 minutes
R39 Keep measurements at full precision and round once, per priced trip, before pricing. Money rounds to cents Now the same transport always prices the same

9. Documentation, exclusions, occupancy and filing

# The system must Level Example

Out of scope

Claim operations (holding, replacing, voiding and appealing claims), each tenant’s own values, deciding who pays, workers’ compensation, and Medicare-Medicaid dual-eligible billing.

How to test a design against them

The 41-axis variation catalogue (redesign-261001/02-variation-catalogue.md) and the 26-payer walk (03-design-overview.md §7) are the test set. Every case must be configurable now or have room reserved. None may need per-payer code. The proposed design below meets them: 7 payers as is, 16 with reserved room, 3 out of scope, and none that cannot be configured.

How a claim gets its payer and billing identity

A billing identity is a legal business the payer pays: name, NPI (national provider number) and tax ID. RideCare bills as OK RIDE CARE and as David Roberts Consulting (DRC). Identities belong to the tenant.

  • A transport carries one billing fact, its payer, and no identity.
  • The payer’s submission configuration in force on the service date names an identity, or is blank and follows the tenant default in force on that date.
  • The claim key is payer, covered client and service date, and the claim stamps the identity.
  • A payer-assigned provider number, a taxonomy override or a contract code describes one identity’s enrolment, so a configuration that sets one must name its identity. Colorado’s 11638 goes only on DRC’s Colorado claims.
  • A new address or phone is a new dated version of the identity. No payer configuration changes.

Versions over time

Every dated row changes by insert, and the editor asks for one date.

  • New versions. Trigger close_prior_version sets the prior row’s effective_to to the new start minus one day and its end_reason to superseded. A version inserted between two others ends the day before the later one.
  • Gaps. dated_versions_contiguous runs at commit. A row may be followed by a gap only when its end_reason is terminated (one configuration ended on purpose) or deactivated (the payer was deactivated).
  • Missing rows. When no row is in force on the service date, the build stops with the code of the missing table, such as NO_SCHEDULE_IN_FORCE.
  • Used rows. Closing a row a billed claim used on or after the new start is refused. The change is then a correction: the wrong row is retracted, and the claims that used it are listed.

Deactivating a payer is dated. billing.deactivate_payer(payer, last_date) ends the schedule and the submission configuration in force on that date, marks them deactivated, and retracts any later versions. It refuses a date earlier than a billed claim’s service date.

Transport’s service date Result
on or before the deactivation date prices and bills as before; routing stays in force, so these claims can still be sent, replaced and voided
after it PAYER_DEACTIVATED
after a reactivation from a later start priced on the new versions; dates between the deactivation and the new start stay a gap

Reactivation inserts new versions from a new start date. Deactivating one configuration alone, a schedule for example, ends that row as terminated.

How a transport is priced

One schedule is in force per payer per date, so resolution returns one row or stops. Anything that decides who pays is settled upstream, before pricing:

Case In the design
TANF its own payer
Colorado the Colorado Access payer’s own schedule
Crisis, requesting entity attribution, decided before pricing. A rate line never tests payer, programme, crisis or requesting entity
One line, many transports transport.priced_unit_id beside transport.claim_id, tied to the same claim by a composite foreign key
Choosing between lines choice groups whose bands are disjoint by constraint, so no order decides a price

Rate lines price measured facts of the transport: distance, wait minutes, extra stops, staff on board, a fuel index, a cancellation stage.

Distance basis

A rider path measures the rider’s own trip: one way or round trip, between the rider’s own pickup and dropoff or from a fixed reference location. A vehicle path measures the vehicle and allocates it to riders. The source is the map.

Distance basis Measures
rider · one_way · client endpoints · map the client’s pickup to dropoff (“curb to curb”)
rider · round_trip · fixed endpoints · map from a named reference location to the far point and back
vehicle · chained · map the vehicle’s driven path along its route, allocated to each transport

vehicle · chained follows the route the way Echo1 computes start_to_end_distance today (transport-leg-distances.ts):

Part Definition
sequence the route’s stops by route_sequence, sequence_number, stop id; a cancelled transport’s stops skipped
approach from the nearest driven stop before the pickup, of any kind, else the route’s starting location
loaded leg pickup to dropoff
return for the route’s last driven transport, when the route carries more than one transport or is the driver’s last route: to the ending location, else the starting location
threshold the mileage line’s threshold, subtracted once from the transport’s whole distance, floor 0
rounding half-up to whole miles, per transport

Legs are kept at full precision and rounded once. A transport with another rider’s stop between its pickup and dropoff fails CHAINED_PATH_OVERLAP, and a transport with no route fails MEASURE_UNAVAILABLE.

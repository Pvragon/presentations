RideCare V2.1 · Billing Encoding

Why a rate schedule is three tables, not one

Two genuine one-to-manys, so two tables past the payer. A single flat row can hold the fixed core of a rate, but not the varying-length list of priced terms hanging off it — that's the whole reason to normalize. Everything that used to be "base params + components + add-ons" is now one uniform rate_line.

payer 1 ──< ∞ rate_schedule 1 ──< ∞ rate_line

01The flatten test

Put a payer's rate on one row and try to store two real contracts side by side. Griffin fills 3 fields; Larned needs 20+, several of them repeating. The flat table can only cope by guessing a column ceiling and drowning in NULLs — or by hiding the lists in a blob SQL can't see.

Breaks three ways. A 6th add-on = schema migration. ~90% NULL. "Which contracts have a fuel surcharge?" means OR-ing addon1..addon5. The blob alternative removes NULLs but is invisible to CHECK / FK / index / RLS / audit.

Bends, doesn't break. One table, one shape. 8 terms = 8 rows, 1 = 1 row, a 6th add-on = one INSERT. Base, tier, band, route, and add-on are all just rate_line rows with a role — every one queryable, constrainable, audit-logged.

02The relationships

Only rate_schedule + rate_line are the new encoding. Everything else already exists in tier-2 — the two symmetric output paths a priced unit lands in: service_line → claim → billing_export (the 837 batch) and invoice_line → invoice (the contract batch). The only net-new columns elsewhere are transport.journey_id and the new invoice_line (the contract mirror of service_line).

The load-bearing idea

Every table earns its place through a real one-to-many — payer→schedules, schedule→lines. Unifying base, tiers, routes, and add-ons into one rate_line means a 6th add-on or a brand-new contract arrives as data (an INSERT), not a schema migration — and the billing engine is one loop instead of three code paths. Three tables is the floor that keeps rates in SQL where they can be constrained, queried, and audited; the only thing below it is a JSON blob that gives all of that up.

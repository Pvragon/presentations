Payer configuration: requirements and proposed design

The capabilities any payer and tenant configuration must have to bill every tenant's contracts in any US state. The proposed design uses one dated schedule per payer, rate lines for every priced term, billing identities chosen per payer, and a stamp on every claim.

Proposed design, draftDesign v0.8.8-draftEcho1 origin/main aa0d1650dUpdated 2026-10-05

The premise

Everything a claim needs, other than the transport itself, comes from four configuration areas. Each is held in its own tables:

  • Pricing: the payer’s rate schedule, dated by service date, and its rate lines. They say what is priced, at what rate, and under which procedure codes and modifiers.
  • Claim terms: the payer’s submission configuration, dated by service date. It inherits a shared claim profile, such as a state’s 837 rules, and names the billing identity.
  • Routing: the payer’s routing configuration, dated by send date. It points at the tenant’s clearinghouse account.
  • Billing identity: the tenant’s legal identities, with a dated default. A tenant can bill under more than one at a time, because payers recognize a new NPI at different times.

Every transport is billed to exactly one payer, decided before billing starts. A claim groups transports, records every setting that built it, and is sent as an 837P or an invoice.

Requirements

What any payer and tenant configuration must do to bill every tenant’s contracts, in any US state. Echo1 decides how to implement them.

Future features are needed by no payer yet. They must be addable without restructuring, and configuration that needs them is refused until they are built.

Whole system

R1. System: configuration, not code. Any tenant sets up any payer through configuration. A new behavior is a new option every tenant can use, never per-payer code.

R2. System: tenant isolation. Each tenant’s data is isolated in the database. A remittance or status response is matched to a claim within its tenant, never by claim number alone.

R3. System: dated versions. The system must support:

  • service dates taken in the transport’s local timezone;
  • terms resolved on the service date, and routing on the send date;
  • changes only by a new version, with no gaps;
  • versions used by a billed claim locked against edits and back-dating.

R4. System: safe pricing. The system must:

  • give exactly one price per transport, refusing conflicting settings on save;
  • stop with a reason when data is missing, never pricing it as zero;
  • price only on the payer and measured facts of the transport, with who pays decided upstream.

R5. System: reproducible claims. Each claim records the configuration, identity and address that built it, and how each charge was derived, and reproduces exactly.

Platform

R6. Platform: shared templates. The system must support:

  • rules common to many payers kept once, as a dated template each payer references (a state’s 837 rules);
  • a payer overriding a single setting of its template without copying the rest;
  • starting templates for rate cards and invoice layouts, copied into a payer and then edited;
  • templates kept by the platform for every tenant, and by a tenant for its own payers.

Tenant

R7. Tenant: billing identities. The system must support:

  • more than one billing identity per tenant in use at the same time: legal name, NPI, tax ID, taxonomy, address;
  • a dated tenant default identity;
  • a new NPI or tax ID as a new identity, and dated address and contact changes on the same one.

R8. Tenant: settings. The system must support:

  • a home timezone, the default for each payer’s period timezone;
  • a claim number (CLM01) on every claim, unique within each clearinghouse account it is sent through, in a form payers do not reformat, and returned on the payment;
  • clearinghouse connections: the tenant’s submitter id and the settings that send claim files to a clearinghouse and fetch its responses.

How a claim gets its payer and billing identity

A billing identity is a legal business the payer pays: name, NPI (national provider number) and tax ID. A tenant can bill under more than one at a time. RideCare is moving from David Roberts Consulting LLC (trading as OK Ride Care) to Ride Care Inc., a new entity with its own NPI, payer by payer as each recognizes the new one.

  • A transport carries one billing fact, its payer, and no identity.
  • The payer’s submission configuration in force on the service date names an identity, or is blank and follows the tenant default in force on that date.
  • The claim key is payer, covered client and service date, and the claim stamps the identity.
  • A payer-assigned provider number, a taxonomy override or a contract code describes one identity’s enrollment, so a configuration that sets one must name its identity.
  • A new address or phone is a new dated version of the identity. No payer configuration changes.

Versions over time

Every dated row changes by insert, and the editor asks for one date.

  • New versions. Trigger close_prior_version sets the prior row’s effective_to to the new start minus one day and its end_reason to superseded. A version inserted between two others ends the day before the later one.
  • Gaps. dated_versions_contiguous runs at commit. A row may be followed by a gap only when its end_reason is terminated (one configuration ended on purpose) or deactivated (the payer was deactivated).
  • Missing rows. When no row is in force on the service date, the transport is not billed and the missing setting is named for review.
  • Used rows. Closing a row a billed claim used on or after the new start is refused. The change is then a correction: the wrong row is retracted, and the claims that used it are listed.

Deactivating a payer is dated. billing.deactivate_payer(payer, last_date) ends the schedule and the submission configuration in force on that date, marks them deactivated, and retracts any later versions. It refuses a date earlier than a billed claim’s service date.

Transport’s service date Result
up to deactivation prices and bills as before; routing stays in force, so these claims can still be sent, replaced and voided
after it not billed; flagged as after the payer’s deactivation
after reactivation priced on the new versions; dates between the deactivation and the new start stay a gap

Reactivation inserts new versions from a new start date. Deactivating one configuration alone, a schedule for example, ends that row as terminated.

How a transport is priced

One schedule is in force per payer per date, so resolution returns one row or stops. Anything that decides who pays is settled upstream, before pricing:

Case In the design
TANF its own payer
Colorado the Colorado Access payer’s own schedule
Crisis, requesting entity attribution, decided before pricing. A rate line never tests payer, program, crisis or requesting entity
One line, many transports transport.priced_unit_id beside transport.claim_id, tied to the same claim by a composite foreign key
Choosing between lines band sets on the rate lines, whose ranges cannot overlap, so no order decides a price

Rate lines price measured facts of the transport: distance, wait minutes, extra stops, staff on board, a fuel index, a cancellation stage.

Distance basis

A rider path measures the rider’s own trip: one way or round trip, between the rider’s own pickup and dropoff or from a fixed reference location. A vehicle path measures the vehicle and allocates it to riders. The source is the map.

Distance basis Measures
rider · one_way · client endpoints · map the client’s pickup to dropoff (“curb to curb”)
rider · round_trip · fixed endpoints · map from a named reference location to the far point and back
vehicle · chained · map the vehicle’s driven path along its route, allocated to each transport

vehicle · chained follows the route the way Echo1 computes start_to_end_distance today (transport-leg-distances.ts):

Part Definition
sequence the route’s stops by route_sequence, sequence_number, stop id; a canceled transport’s stops skipped
approach from the nearest driven stop before the pickup, of any kind, else the route’s starting location
loaded leg pickup to dropoff
return for the route’s last driven transport, when the route carries more than one transport or is the driver’s last route: to the ending location, else the starting location
threshold the mileage line’s threshold, subtracted once from the transport’s whole distance, floor 0
rounding half-up to whole miles, per transport

Legs are kept at full precision and rounded once. A transport with another rider’s stop between its pickup and dropoff, or with no route, is not priced and is flagged for review.

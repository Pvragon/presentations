RideCare · Versioned Payer Configuration — Proposal

payer → rate_schedule / payer_edi_config → rate_line

RideCare has multiple payers, multiple 837/EDI configurations, and multiple rate schedules. These get mixed and matched, and all of them are guaranteed to change over time in a way that must be tracked. A payer's rate today is not its rate last year, and its EDI setup changes on a completely different schedule than its rates do.

So this is a proposal for versioned payer configuration — rates are just the first instance of the pattern. It covers payer rate and 837/EDI configuration encoding only; it does not change 837 filing itself.

transport N ──1 payer 1 ──< ∞ rate_schedule 1 ──< ∞ rate_line
payer 1 ──< ∞ payer_edi_config

01The model

Four tables. payer is stable identity — never versioned. rate_schedule and payer_edi_config are versioned, effective-dated children a payer owns many of over time; rate_line holds the terms of one rate_schedule version.

⤢click to zoom

Resolution reads downward: transport → payer → rate_schedule (the version whose effective range covers the service date) → rate_line. Every foreign key below points the other way — upward, child to parent.

FK direction — read down, point up

A payer owns MANY rate_schedule rows over time (a 2024 rate, a 2025 rate, a 2026 rate) and MANY payer_edi_config rows over time, so the foreign key cannot live on payer — it lives on the versioned child:

  • transport.payer_id → payer
  • rate_schedule.payer_id → payer FK on child
  • rate_line.rate_schedule_id → rate_schedule FK on child
  • payer_edi_config.payer_id → payer FK on child

payer stores no list and has no rate_schedule_id column — resolution walks down by querying the child for the row whose effective range covers today (or the service date), never by following a pointer stored on the parent.

Why a separate table from rate_schedule: EDI settings and rates change on different clocks. A clearinghouse payer-ID change is unrelated to a rate renegotiation — versioning them together would spawn a false new rate version every time the EDI side changes, and vice versa.

03Versioning

  • A change is an INSERT of a new row — never edit a rate_schedule, rate_line, or payer_edi_config row that has already been billed against.
  • Close the prior row's effective_to, then insert the new row. effective_to NULL means current.
  • Resolution is by service date, not export date — a December ride billed in February prices at December's rate.
  • Recording the resolved id on the record at pricing time makes history provable rather than merely re-derivable.

04Templates

No extra table. payer_id IS NULL on rate_schedule (and on payer_edi_config) marks an unattached, named template — e.g. "OK Facility Standard: $45/trip + $2.25/mi over 30", or a BASE-837 config.

  • The UI's "start from…" list shows the NULL-payer_id rows and copies the schedule plus its rate_line rows, setting payer_id on the copy.
  • Resolution always filters by payer_id, so a template can never be billed against — it has no payer to resolve from.
  • Copying is a one-time snapshot: editing a template later does not propagate to payers already created from it. That's correct under immutability — the UI states this plainly rather than leaving it implicit.

05How users see it

Users never see four tables or manage effective dates directly.

screenbehind it
Payer detailidentity + current rate panel + current 837 setup panel — payer and its two current-effective children
Rate editorone price list, add/remove terms — rate_schedule + rate_line
History tabread-only timeline of past rates / 837 configs — the non-current rows

Changing a rate is a "New rate, effective [date]" action — it closes the old row and inserts the new one; the user never types an effective_to directly.

06Three billable grains

rate_schedule.billable_grain decides what the biller iterates — independent of the rate math.

grainmeaning
person_legone client A→B
vehicle_tripone vehicle trip, 1..N patients, ONE unit — billed per trip, not per person (e.g. Sedgwick: "per trip, not per person charge (one or more patients)")
frequencyone payer × one period, fixed charge

08Audit trail

  • The 837 file itself is already a decent audit record of what was billed.
  • Ideally transport_history also receives an audit write whenever a transport's resolved configuration resolves or changes — a record of what was resolved, not just what was filed.
  • Denormalized stamping — recording the resolved rate_schedule_id / payer_edi_config_id on the record at pricing/export time — is acceptable where it makes history provable rather than merely re-derivable from effective-date ranges.

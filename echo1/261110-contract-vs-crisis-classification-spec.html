RideCare · Echo1 · Billing classification

Contract vs crisis classification for Echo1 rides

The exact procedure for choosing an Echo1 ride's payer: insurance as a crisis ride, or a contract customer.

Version 1.5.0

This procedure decides, for each Echo1 ride, who is billed: insurance (a crisis ride) or a contract customer. Follow the steps in order. In Step 5 and Step 7, the first rule that applies decides the class; Step 6 may then adjust the contract name, as it says. Where this page gives a field, a list or a pattern, use it exactly. Do not substitute a similar field, add synonyms or relax a match.

The method this is based on

This page implements Jaime’s retrospective classification ladder of 2026-11-04 (diagram below). It builds on his forward billing-classification process (the 9/4 walkthrough, “How a ride gets classified for billing”, on this site): crisis outranks the form, and a crisis ride is defined by the state’s 43A documentation.

What changed on 2026-11-10

The steps below follow the diagram, with these rulings applied:

  • This classification chooses the ride’s Payer. The Payer field already on a ride is never an input. It is recorded and compared with the result, and any disagreement goes to a review list. The dedicated request form decides the contract if and only if the ride has no crisis signal.
  • Crisis answer Yes also covers a mobile crisis team response. The court-ordered answer on the form is still not a crisis by itself; a court order document on file is.
  • The ODMHSAS Discharge form (ODMHDIS) is a dedicated form like the others. It resolves to ODMHSAS, which bills to the ODMHSAS Central PO from July 2026.
  • Every out-of-state ride is a contract ride (both ends outside Oklahoma), crisis or not, even where the contract covers only crisis rides, as in Nebraska. None is an Oklahoma Medicaid ride.
  • ODMHDIS rides before July 2026 are grouped as one billed contract for review. OCRU stays OCRU; its roll-up to the Central PO happens later in billing.
  • A contract starts on its start date (v1.5). A source names a contract only for rides on or after the date that contract’s first agreement began (Table 4). Earlier rides on that contract’s form or facilities are not contract rides; they fall through to the next source and usually end as crisis (insurance).
  • Stage 1, step 4 in the diagram (“destination on the crisis-facility list”) was drawn as a placeholder and marked NOT USED, because no such list exists. It stays unused, so Stage 1 has three working checks: the crisis answer, a crisis document, a crisis keyword.

How to read the patterns on this page

  • Every pattern is Python re syntax, applied with re.search (it matches anywhere in the text, not only the whole text).
  • “Any case” means the re.IGNORECASE flag. “Capitals only” means no flag.
  • \b is a word boundary. In PostgreSQL write it as \y, because \b means something else there and the pattern would silently never match.
  • Inside the tables, a | in a pattern is written \| only because markdown tables need it. The pattern itself contains a plain | (alternation). For example (3rd\|third) means (3rd|third).
  • A text value that is NULL, empty, or only spaces counts as missing.

The rule

This classification chooses the ride’s Payer. A ride is a crisis ride if it has a crisis signal. If it has none, the request form decides the contract, then the fallback sources in Step 3. The Payer field already on the ride is never an input; it is only compared with the result (Step 8).

Scope

  • Unit: one row of transport. Request-level inputs (answers, the Payer field, the form, notes on the request, request attachments) are shared by every transport on the same request.
  • Rides covered: transports whose request has transport_request.submission_number >= 300000.
  • Rides skipped: QA rides, where the client’s full name matches \mqa\M in PostgreSQL (client.name_full ~* '\mqa\M', client joined on client.id = transport_request.client_id). Skip them entirely.
  • Not covered: V1-era rides (submission number below 300000). They lack the Echo1 intake answers this procedure reads.
  • What this decides: the billing class and the contract customer. It does not decide whether a ride is billable (completed, not voided, not cancelled). That check is separate and does not change any step here, including the voters in Step 4.

Step 2. Work out the crisis signals

A ride has a crisis signal if Signal A, B or C below is present.

Signal A: the crisis answer is Yes

The crisis answer is Yes if any of these is true: crisis answer 1, crisis answer 2, mobile crisis team.

The crisis answer is No if crisis answer 1 is false and none of the three is true.

Otherwise the crisis answer is Unanswered.

The court-ordered answer is never part of the crisis answer. A court-ordered transport is not a crisis by itself.

Signal B: a crisis document is on file

The state’s 43A rules define a crisis ride by its documentation. Five documents qualify: a peace officer’s written statement, an emergency order of detention (EOD), a licensed mental health professional (LMHP) statement, a court order, and a third-party statement.

A crisis document is on file if at least one attachment’s file_name matches this pattern, any case:

lmhp|crisis\s*petition|petition|court|orders?\b|\beod\b|detention|emergency\s*order|peace|officer|third|affidavit|43\s*-?\s*a

Legal basis, as researched for the forward process (spec v0.15.0, 2026-10-04, in docs/investigations/260716-billing-classification/archive/): 43A O.S. § 5-207(C) (the peace officer’s written statement), § 5-208 (the licensed professional’s statement, and the emergency detention that follows the initial assessment), and OAC 317:30-5-348(a) (Medicaid transport of a member “reasonably believed to be experiencing a behavioral health crisis”, for “examination, emergency detention, protective custody, or inpatient services”). The court order that qualifies is a court order for inpatient treatment or evaluation for treatment. The state’s transportation guidance: https://oklahoma.gov/odmhsas/treatment/comprehensive-crisis-response/transportation.html

A court order document is a crisis document; only the court-ordered answer is excluded. That checkbox (is_court_ordered) is a flag, not a document, and it also marks court orders that are not behavioral health crises, such as custody exchanges and unaccompanied minors. This pattern is deliberately loose and is kept exactly as the 11/04 ladder used it. It also matches words such as “courtesy” or “recorders”, and \beod\b misses EOD_scan.pdf because _ is a word character. Do not tighten or loosen it.

Signal C: a crisis keyword is in the notes

A crisis keyword is present if the Notes text matches any of these patterns.

Step 3. Work out the contract candidate

Check these sources in order and stop at the first one that names a contract. The result is the ride’s contract candidate, together with the source that named it. The Payer field already on the ride is not a source.

  1. Request form. If re.requester_id is in Table 2 (exact, case-sensitive, no trimming), that contract.
  2. Both ends. If the pickup facility name and the dropoff facility name both match the same contract (see “Matching a name” below), that contract.
  3. One end. If only one end matches a contract, that contract. If the two ends match different contracts, use the pickup’s.
  4. Referrer. If the Referrer text matches a contract, that contract.
  5. Email domain. If the submitter email domain is in the email-domain map (Step 4), that contract.
  6. Known contact. If the submitter name is in the known-contact map (Step 4), that contract.
  7. Otherwise there is no contract candidate.

A facility end never matches when its facility_type = 'private_residence' or its facility_name is missing.

Contract start. A source names a contract only if the ride date is on or after that contract’s start date in Table 4. If the ride date is earlier, that source names nothing: go on to the next source. A contract not in Table 4 has no start limit. Table 4 holds start dates only; end dates are left out on purpose, because renewals were not always filed and an end date would wrongly cut off rides.

Matching a name. Test the Veterans Affairs pattern first: veterans?\s*(affairs|admin|medical)|\bva\s*(medical|hospital|health)|\bvamc\b|dole\s*va, any case. If it matches, the contract is “Wichita VA” when the name contains wichita or dole (any case) or, for a facility, its state_code is KS; otherwise it is “Oklahoma VA”. The Referrer has no state code. Only if the VA pattern does not match, test the name against every pattern in Table 3, any case. If exactly one contract’s pattern matches, that is the contract. If none or more than one matches, there is no match.

Step 4. Build the email-domain and known-contact maps

Build both maps once per run, before classifying.

Voters. Every in-scope, non-QA ride (Scope), billable or not, whose request form is in Table 2, except ODMHDIS. Each voter counts as one vote for its form’s contract. Use the same date range as the rides being classified.

  • Email-domain map. Count votes per submitter email domain. Skip a missing domain, a free-mail domain (gmail.com, yahoo.com, hotmail.com, outlook.com, icloud.com, aol.com, live.com, msn.com, att.net, cox.net, sbcglobal.net), and any domain matching ridecare|okridecare|pvragon|davidroberts|drc (any case). Map a domain to a contract only if it has at least 3 votes and at least 80% of its votes are for that one contract. Never map a domain to Lincoln County or Diversion Hub, because other organizations use their forms.
  • Known-contact map. Count votes per submitter name, skipping a missing name. Map a name to a contract only if it has at least 3 votes and at least 90% of its votes are for that one contract.

Step 5. Decide the class

Apply these rules in order. The first one that applies decides the ride.

  1. Out-of-state contract. If the contract candidate is one of these out-of-state contracts, the result is CONTRACT, that contract, step id A. This applies even to a crisis ride, because out-of-state rides are never eligible for Oklahoma Medicaid. - Texas Recovery Center (Regard Healthcare) - South Central Louisiana HSA - NE (payer pending) - Sedgwick County / COMCARE (KS) - Wichita VA - Coffeyville Regional Medical Center (KS) - Larned State Hospital (KS)
  2. Both ends out of state. Applies only if both the pickup state and the dropoff state are present and neither is OK. An out-of-state ride is never an Oklahoma Medicaid ride, so it is always a contract ride, crisis or not, even where the state’s contract covers only crisis rides (as in Nebraska). The result is CONTRACT, step id B: - the contract candidate from Step 3; - else, if both ends are NE, NE (payer pending); - else Missing contract (<pickup state>/<dropoff state>), for example Missing contract (KS/KS).
  3. Crisis answer Yes: CRISIS, step id S1-1.
  4. Crisis document on file. If the crisis answer is No, go to Step 7. Otherwise CRISIS, step id S1-2.
  5. Crisis keyword in the notes. If the crisis answer is No, go to Step 7. Otherwise CRISIS, step id S1-3.
  6. Contract candidate. CONTRACT, that contract. The step id names the source: S2-1 (form), S2-2 (both ends), S2-3 (one end), S2-4 (referrer), S2-5 (email domain), S2-6 (known contact).
  7. Otherwise go to Step 7.

Rules 3 to 5 come before rule 6 because crisis outranks the form: for a ride with an end in Oklahoma, the form decides the contract only when there are no crisis signals.

Step 6. Name the contract customer to bill

This step runs only for a CONTRACT result from Step 5. Call the Step 5 contract the ladder contract; this step produces the billed contract.

  1. ODMHSAS from July 2026. If the ladder contract’s name starts with ODMHSAS, from any source, and the ride date is on or after 2026-07-01, the billed contract is ODMHSAS Central - non-crisis (PO 4529070498).
  2. ODMHSAS Discharge before July 2026. If the ladder contract is exactly ODMHSAS (from the ODMHDIS form) and the ride date is before 2026-07-01, the billed contract is ODMHDIS pre-July 2026. These rides are grouped together for review; the form does not identify the facility.
  3. Otherwise the billed contract is the ladder contract. OCRU is an ODMHSAS facility, but a ride whose Payer field is OCRU is compared against OCRU; rolling OCRU into the Central PO happens later in the billing workflow.

Step 7. Rides no rule has decided

A ride reaches this step when it has no contract candidate and no crisis signal, or when a crisis document or keyword conflicts with a crisis answer of No.

  1. If a crisis document is on file: CRISIS, step id D1 crisis document.
  2. Otherwise, if either end is a private residence (facility_type = 'private_residence') or has a missing facility name: HOLD, reason unknown contract, step id D2 private residence. RideCare may self-fund these.
  3. Otherwise: CRISIS, step id D3 facility to facility.

Note the consequence of rule 3: a ride whose crisis answer is No, with a weak keyword such as ER in the notes, ends as CRISIS here even when a contract form or facility is present. That follows the rules as ruled (2026-11-10).

disagrees_with_payer. Let the Payer contract be Table 1’s contract for p.payer_name when p.payer_type = 'contract', and nothing otherwise. Then the result is true when any of these holds:

  • the Payer contract exists and the class is CRISIS or HOLD;
  • the Payer contract exists, the class is CONTRACT, and the Payer contract differs from the ladder contract. Two contracts whose names both start with ODMHSAS count as the same for this comparison;
  • the Payer contract does not exist and the class is CONTRACT;
  • p.payer_type = 'contract' but p.payer_name is not in Table 1.

Otherwise it is false.

A CRISIS ride is billed to insurance: SoonerCare or the MCO from the coverage check, and DMH if there is none. Every ride with disagrees_with_payer = true goes on a review list. Never change the Payer field automatically.

How a ride gets classified for billing

How RideCare decides what kind of ride a request is and who pays for it, from the request to the bill.

Reviewed with the team 2026-08-28Process v0.10.0Updated 2026-10-06

At a glance

Every ride is classified for billing when the request comes in, before it runs.

From the form alone: Crisis if 43a evidence is attached or attested, Contracted if it came in on a Requesting Entity’s own form, otherwise Reject.

What is changing, and why

The state changed what a crisis ride is. Until this spring, Crisis and Secured meant the same thing in the app: a ride in a secured vehicle was billed as crisis. The state now requires documentation (a peace officer’s written statement, an EOD, an LMHP statement, a court order or a third-party statement) for a ride to count as crisis. A secured ride is no longer automatically a crisis ride. We decide on the evidence, before the ride goes out.

The state ended prescheduled rides under the old arrangement. In their place we have individual contracts (ODMHSAS facilities, Oceans, the county, out-of-state contracts), each with its own payment terms. A non-crisis ride has to be billed to a specific contract, and nothing on a request says which one. A prescheduled ride no contract covers is not approved.

The new fields

Field What it means Who sets it
Billing Class One of four: Crisis / Contracted / Self-Funded (DRC) / Reject. There is no “not sure” option. System proposes; dispatcher / eligibility checker confirms
Crisis Confirmation Status For crisis rides only: Confirmed (paperwork in hand) / Provisional (went out on a manager’s approval, paperwork still owed) / Unconfirmed (paperwork not in hand, no manager decision yet) / Documentation Failed (chased, never obtained) / N/A Dispatcher, manager, processing
Manager review A flag on any request, of any class. While it is on, the ride is not Ready and waits for a lead or manager. Dispatcher, or a rule the requesting entity set up; a lead or manager clears it
Insurance (as submitted) The insurance and policy number the person filling out the form told us. Recorded as given. The submitter
Payer Who we actually bill. This is the field billing runs on. Eligibility check sets a starting value; billing makes the final pick

Two other fields stay as they are because they are operational: Secured / Unsecured and Prescheduled. Secured / Unsecured is how we route the vehicle: every crisis ride is secured, and a secured ride can be a non-crisis ride with a risk flag. Prescheduled is whether the ride is for now or later: contract rides tend to be prescheduled and Medicaid crisis rides tend not to be.

The process, from submission to billing

1A request comes in

Two kinds of form. A Requesting Entity’s own form (a contract group’s dedicated link) takes either a crisis ride or a prescheduled ride; the group submits everything through their one link. The website form is crisis-only.

A ride that isn’t a crisis needs at least four hours’ notice. Its requested pickup time has to be at least four hours after the request comes in, or the request goes back to the submitter. Only a crisis ride can be asked for right now, and a crisis ride can still be scheduled for later.

The submitter tells us the client’s insurance and policy number. If it’s a crisis, they upload the 43a paperwork or answer Yes to the crisis question. Answering Yes makes the form’s closing statement read:

You reasonably believe this person is experiencing a behavioral health crisis and needs secure transportation for examination, emergency detention, protective custody or inpatient treatment. To the best of your knowledge, one of the following exists or is being obtained: a peace officer’s written statement, an emergency order of detention, a licensed mental health professional’s statement, a court order, or a third-party statement. You understand that RideCare requires that document before it can bill for this transport. If it is not provided, your organization may be responsible for the cost of this transport. This attestation is your own statement as the requesting party.

When a document can’t be obtained, the attestation is accepted; on its own it sends the ride to manager review.

The system then proposes a classification, in this order:

  • 43a evidence present or attested → propose Crisis. Crisis outranks the form: a crisis ride on a contract form is still a crisis ride.
  • Otherwise, arrived on a Requesting Entity’s own form → propose Contracted, with that contract as the payer.
  • Otherwise (website or general form, no crisis) → propose Reject and bounce it.

Self-Funded is never proposed by the system; a person decides that.

Some requesting entities also set up rules that send a request to manager review as it arrives. One example is a trip that goes more than a set number of miles past a state line.

2Two checks, at the same time, by different people

  • Eligibility check: will we get paid? The dispatcher or eligibility checker sees the system’s proposal and its reasons, runs the coverage lookup, and confirms the Billing Class. This is where the class becomes final. The payer picked here is a starting point. Billing makes the final pick before the ride is billed.
  • QA check: can we run this ride? Right address, right time, right vehicle, real person on the other end.

When both are done the request reads as Ready and can be dispatched.

3The eligibility check

Which kind of ride

  • Crisis: the 43a evidence is there, or the crisis signals are strong and the paperwork is on its way. Needs a Crisis Confirmation Status and a Medicaid payer.
  • Contracted: arrived on a Requesting Entity’s own form. The payer is that entity’s payer.
  • Self-Funded (DRC): no crisis, no contract, but a veteran or a child, and RideCare chooses to serve. A lead or manager has to approve it, with a note saying whether the rider is a veteran or a child.
  • Reject: none of the above, including a contract ride sent through the website. The dispatcher phones the submitter and asks them to resubmit on the group’s own link. Only a lead or manager may instead classify it as a contract ride. They write a note, and it is recorded as an exception.

Manager review

Any request can go to manager review, whatever its class. The dispatcher sends it there when they can’t decide. A rule the requesting entity set up can send it there too, for example a long trip across a state line. A crisis request with no paperwork goes there as it arrives. While it waits, the ride is not Ready. A lead or manager decides, promptly. That means anyone who holds the manager-review permission. Every decision needs a written note.

The manager lands the request on one of the four answers. If it’s a crisis whose paperwork isn’t in hand yet, the manager can approve it provisionally: the ride goes out, flagged so processing knows to chase the paperwork afterwards. If the ride needs no paperwork for a reason the manager knows to be good, a ride to a first assessment being the usual one, the manager confirms it as such and nothing is chased. The dispatcher may mark a request as a likely first assessment to help; only the manager’s decision counts.

Who gets the bill

The Billing Class tells us which list to look in; one more step picks the name:

  • Crisis → the insurance eligibility check. The submitted coverage is checked against the state’s Medicaid eligibility portal; the payer is whatever it returns, the state Medicaid agency or the member’s managed care organization (MCO). Crisis rides are never billed to private insurance today. In Colorado the portal names the Regional Accountable Entity of the member’s primary care provider, and that’s the payer. In Oklahoma, if the check finds mental health coverage only, or finds no member, the payer is the DMH payer and the ride still runs. If the answer names no payer, the dispatcher leaves the payer blank unless the answer makes it obvious. The ride still runs, and billing picks the payer before the ride is billed.
  • Contracted → the contract that owns the form: submitting on a group’s own link is that group taking responsibility for the ride. Each form is its own billing entity; forms that bill to one party share a billing group (ODMHSAS is the main example).
  • Self-Funded → RideCare (DRC).
  • Reject → no payer.

The record of each proposal and confirmation holds the facts behind it. When a person changed it by hand, it holds who did it. A manager’s decision also holds their written note.

4Ready → dispatched → the ride

Once both checks are done the ride is dispatched and run as usual.

5After the ride: processing and the paperwork chase

For most rides, processing certifies the record and releases it to billing.

For a crisis ride that went out provisionally, processing works from a searchable list of every such ride and chases the paperwork through three levels before anything is written off:

  1. Processing: the driver got the document during the ride, or dispatch obtains it from the submitter.
  2. The eligibility specialist: follows up directly with the case worker or referring agency.
  3. Final review (Jeff): the last look before a write-off. The document turns up, or an exception is approved with a written note, or it’s marked Documentation Failed and written off as non-billable.

Every provisional ride ends certified, either confirmed or written off. From 2026-10-01 the new state contract lets the state demand the 43a documents before paying.

6Billing

Billing makes the final payer pick. Where the dispatcher left the payer blank, billing picks one. Where one was picked, billing confirms or changes it. Then billing sends the ride out on the right path: 837P claim for a Medicaid agency or MCO, invoice for a contract payer. What the submitter originally told us about the client’s insurance stays on the record untouched.
